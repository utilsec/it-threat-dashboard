Top Story

N-able N-central auth-bypass exploited for admin takeover lands in CISA KEV — the RMM platform that reaches across MSP-managed estates

CISA added CVE-2026-18577, an authentication-bypass flaw in N-able’s N-central remote monitoring and management (RMM) platform, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 3 August after confirming active exploitation. N-able says a remote, unauthenticated attacker can bypass authentication and seize administrative control of vulnerable N-central servers; once in, observed attackers abused the platform’s Take Control feature to reach managed endpoints and stood up a Cloudflare Tunnel for persistence. The flaw is an incomplete fix for the earlier CVE-2026-18576, and exploitation was seen from around 1 August against both hosted and on-premises deployments. N-central is widely used by managed service providers and enterprise IT to administer infrastructure across many customer environments, so a compromised server is a pre-authenticated path into downstream networks — an RMM supply-chain exposure, not an isolated bug. Upgrade to N-central 2026.3.1 Hotfix 1 (2026.3.1.7) immediately, pull consoles off the public internet, rotate credentials, and hunt for the published IoCs (a ‘Cloudflared’ service and svchost.exe in Documents).

High Incident

Iran-linked cyberattacks disrupt municipal water systems across roughly seven states

A wave of cyberattacks hit U.S. municipal water and wastewater utilities across about seven states in late July, with Minnesota hardest hit — reporting disruptions at more than 30 systems — and Georgia and Michigan later disclosing intrusions. Some operators were forced to abandon digital controls and revert to manual operation. The FBI and EPA issued a joint advisory; investigators cite tradecraft consistent with prior Iranian operations, though formal attribution is not confirmed, and no ransom was demanded — suggesting disruption itself was the goal. Water utilities sit at the IT/OT boundary, and the same edge-exposure and weak-authentication patterns threaten any enterprise where corporate networks meet operational systems. Inventory internet-exposed HMIs/PLCs and remote access, enforce MFA and strong credentials, segment IT from OT, and review the joint advisory’s guidance.

Medium Incident

Semiconductor maker Analog Devices confirms data breach after files stolen

Analog Devices, a major U.S. semiconductor manufacturer, disclosed in an SEC filing that an intruder gained unauthorized access and stole certain files. The company detected the unauthorized access on 23 June and says the incident did not disrupt operations and is not expected to have a material financial impact. The extortion group ExfilSquad publicly claimed the theft of about 570,000 records in late July, though it had removed the listing from its leak site by the time of reporting. Data-theft extortion of large manufacturers and their intellectual property remains a steady pressure; organizations should validate exfiltration monitoring, review third-party and identity access, and rehearse breach-notification workflows.

Medium Research

Cisco Talos: phishing is now behind more than half of intrusions as evasion sharpens

Cisco Talos’ latest incident-response trends (covering roughly March–June 2026) found phishing was the initial access vector in over half of investigated incidents — up from about a third the prior quarter. Attackers leaned on QR codes embedded in PDF attachments to slip past email gateways, credential-harvesting pages hosted on trusted platforms like Microsoft 365 and SharePoint, and phishing-as-a-service kits that abuse OAuth device-authorization flows and refresh tokens to defeat MFA. Talos also flagged a persistent QR-code campaign (tracked as UAT-11764) using victim-tailored PDFs. Reinforce phishing-resistant MFA, inspect QR/PDF content, monitor for anomalous OAuth device-code grants, and refresh user awareness.

Critical Advisory

SonicWall SMA1000 hit by two actively-exploited zero-days — a CVSS-10.0 SSRF chainable to remote code execution

SonicWall issued an urgent patch for two zero-days in its SMA1000 secure-access appliances after Rapid7’s MDR team found them exploited in the wild. CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0) is an unauthenticated server-side request forgery in the Workplace interface that lets an attacker force the appliance to make network requests of their choosing; CVE-2026-15410 (CVSS 7.2) is a code-injection flaw in the Appliance Management Console that normally requires authentication — but the two can be chained for unauthenticated remote code execution. CISA added both to its KEV catalog on 14 July with a 17 July federal deadline. SMA appliances front remote access to internal resources, so a compromise is a direct route inside. Upgrade to the fixed builds (12.4.3-03453+ / 12.5.0-02835+) now, remove management-interface exposure, and hunt for compromise.

High Advisory

Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center hard-coded credential exploited in the wild — now in CISA KEV

Cisco confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3) in its Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC), the console that centrally administers Cisco firewalls. The flaw is a hard-coded credential for a low-privileged account that lets an unauthenticated, remote attacker log in and access sensitive data; Cisco rated its security impact High because it can be chained with other FMC bugs to escalate privileges. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 29 July with a 1 August federal deadline, and Cisco shipped fixes on 30 July along with IoCs. Because FMC controls firewall policy across an estate, its compromise can undermine perimeter defenses broadly. Apply the fix now, keep the management interface off the public internet, and check for the published IoCs.

High Advisory

Microsoft’s July Patch Tuesday clears a record ~622 fixes, including two actively-exploited zero-days

Microsoft’s July 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed a record-setting ~622 vulnerabilities, among them two zero-days already exploited in attacks: CVE-2026-56155, a privilege-escalation flaw in Active Directory Federation Services that can yield administrator rights, and CVE-2026-56164, an unauthenticated network-based privilege-escalation bug in SharePoint Server. The release also fixed CVE-2026-50661, a BitLocker security-feature bypass that was publicly disclosed before patching (physical-access vector), plus several critical RCEs including a CVSS-9.9 Windows VMSwitch flaw (CVE-2026-57092) and a critical SharePoint bug (CVE-2026-50522). AD FS and SharePoint underpin enterprise identity and collaboration, so prioritize those two exploited zero-days first, then the critical RCEs, across the estate.

House votes to extend the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 for another decade

The U.S. House narrowly passed the FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act (HR 8800) by 216–212, a package that would reauthorize the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 through 2036. The law provides liability, antitrust and FOIA protections for companies that voluntarily share cyber-threat indicators and defensive measures with the government and each other — the legal backbone many enterprises rely on to participate in information-sharing programs. The authority lapsed briefly before short-term extensions and is set to expire again on 30 September 2026 absent action; the measure still needs the Senate. Security and legal teams should track the outcome, since a lapse reintroduces uncertainty over the protections that underpin threat-intelligence sharing. No action required yet — this is pending legislation, not enacted law.
