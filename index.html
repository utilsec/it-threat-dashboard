Top Story

Backdoored ScreenConnect clients are spreading malware to every machine they touch — ConnectWise has confirmed the file-transfer flaw, but as of this edition there is no CVE and no patch

Huntress reported worm-like activity through ConnectWise ScreenConnect after finding the same intrusion pattern on unrelated endpoints in several different customer organisations, with the first incidents detected on 20 August 2026 and a third confirmed on 24 August. The mechanism is the part worth understanding: a modified ScreenConnect client watches for new connections and automatically queues file-transfer messages that push and execute the same four VBScript files — 1.vbs through 4.vbs, which profile the system, stage a payload, retrieve an encrypted second stage matched to that profile, and decrypt and run the final stage — onto each newly connected endpoint, with a further script establishing persistence through a Run registry key. That turns a remote-support tool into a self-propagating delivery channel that inherits the trust the tool already has. ConnectWise published an advisory on 3 September describing an issue in file-transfer behaviour in ScreenConnect Remote Access, Support and Access sessions, affecting both cloud-hosted and on-premises deployments, and said a CVE identifier and an official fix would follow within the week; as of 7 September neither had been issued. Shadowserver counts roughly 6,000 internet-exposed ScreenConnect instances, which is not a vulnerable-population figure and does not include cloud tenants. This leads the page rather than the SonicWall or Chrome zero-days because it is confirmed in-the-wild abuse, in tooling that sits in almost every managed IT estate, with no vendor fix to apply — and because unlike those, the mitigation is a permission change you can make in minutes.

The StyleSmuggler zero-day is still backdooring Magento and Adobe Commerce stores — four days on there is still no CVE, no advisory and no vendor patch

Dutch e-commerce security firm Sansec published an advisory on 5 September reporting active exploitation of a previously unknown flaw in Magento Open Source and Adobe Commerce, which it named StyleSmuggler. The described chain manipulates style properties inside unauthenticated GraphQL queries to inject PHP into files that Magento itself generates, then triggers execution through the platform’s payment-failure reminder email function, giving unauthenticated remote code execution and a persistent backdoor. Sansec reports current releases affected, including 2.4.7, 2.4.8 and 2.4.9, and says instances carrying the August 2026 updates were still compromised. That combination — confirmed exploitation, no vendor fix, no identifier to track against — is the worst state a widely deployed application can be in. Anyone whose organisation runs a storefront, including one owned by marketing or inherited with a subsidiary, is in scope, and unofficial community patches should be treated as risk-managed stopgaps validated in a test environment, not as vendor-supported fixes.

China-nexus Fire Ant is running espionage out of the Cisco routers and TACACS servers it compromised — and deleting the logs you would use to find it

Sygnia published research on 1 September 2026 describing new activity by the China-nexus actor it tracks as Fire Ant, which it overlaps with UNC3886, the group Mandiant has attributed activity to since 2022. Having previously targeted virtualisation infrastructure, the group has moved to network devices: it compromised Cisco IOS XR routers, captured and exfiltrated traffic from multiple devices to external servers, modified the routers for easier management and persistence, and used the compromised estate to build a view of victim networks from several vantage points at once. Critical Incident — Active exploitation

MikroTik routers are being taken over through exposed SSH — CERT Polska names the chain MikroTrick and gives you a one-line indicator to check for

CERT Polska warned that MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities are being actively exploited against devices with SSH reachable from the internet, with attacks occurring since at least 2 September 2026. Six issues were disclosed; the chain being used, which the CERT calls MikroTrick, combines CVE-2026-67276, an SSH authentication bypass rated CVSS 9.2, with CVE-2026-86060, an SSH privilege escalation through a crafted username also rated 9.2, to give an unauthenticated attacker full control of the device. A third flaw, CVE-2026-67277, is a memory disclosure issue in the bandwidth-test service rated 8.8. Fixes are in RouterOS 6.49.21, 7.23.4, 7.24.2 and 7.25beta3. The useful part of the advisory is the detection guidance: log entries showing a login failure for user “-2”, or an account added by ssh:-2@<ip>, indicate exploitation attempts, and the presence of a privileged account named “ops” indicates the device is already compromised. CERT Polska named 82.192.72.4 and 103.102.31.18 among the attacking sources. Cybernews reported roughly 122,500 exposed routers; patching alone does not evict an attacker who already has an account.

High Incident — Identity federation

Around 5,000 Dropbox accounts were taken over without a password — the flaw was in Lenovo ID, the identity provider on the other side of the federation

Dropbox said roughly 5,000 accounts were compromised through a flaw in Lenovo ID, the single sign-on identity provider used to access them. Reporting describes an authentication weakness that allowed account access without the password, over a window of about seventeen days, with accounts that had no multi-factor authentication enabled worst affected. Neither the storage platform nor the customer did anything wrong at the point of failure — the trust decision was delegated to an external identity provider, and that provider’s login flow was broken. This is the shape of federated-identity risk that rarely appears on a risk register: your access control is only as strong as the weakest identity provider you have agreed to trust, your logs show a successful federated login rather than an attack, and your ability to detect it depends on session and device anomalies rather than failed passwords. MFA on the relying party is what limited the damage here, which is the practical takeaway.

Enforce it on your side wherever the platform allows it, because that is the control that survives an IdP failure. Then check you can actually detect this class of event: alert on federated sign-ins from new devices, impossible-travel patterns and unusual data access volumes, since a broken login flow produces successful authentications, not failed ones. High Incident — Mass web compromise

More than 5,400 hacked websites are serving ClickFix lures whose payloads live in blockchain smart contracts — there is no takedown target

Netskope reported a campaign using more than 5,400 compromised websites, mostly WordPress and PrestaShop installations, to deliver ClickFix payloads. What makes it durable is where the next stage is kept: the injected scripts fetch their payload from smart contracts on the BNB Smart Chain testnet, a technique known as EtherHiding. Because the code sits on a blockchain, there is no hosting provider to notify and no domain to sinkhole, and the operators can change the payload at any time without touching the compromised sites. Early variants used the familiar fake CAPTCHA prompt that persuades a user to paste a PowerShell command into a Run dialog; later ones added a WebRTC data-channel stager that opens a covert encrypted channel for attacker-supplied code. Netskope observed a peak of 536 newly infected sites in a single day in August 2026, with around 300 infected sites contacting its endpoints daily. The exposure runs both ways: your own marketing sites may be among the compromised, and your users are the ones being asked to run the command.

Medium Incident — IoT campaign

Operation CameraSwarm compromised more than 14,500 Dahua devices in a 35-day campaign — building infrastructure, not stealing footage

Threat intelligence firm Hunt.io documented a campaign it named Operation CameraSwarm in which more than 14,500 Dahua cameras and recorders were compromised over roughly 35 days. The methods were unglamorous and effective: credential stuffing against devices still using default or reused passwords, authentication bypasses in legacy firmware, and abuse of the peer-to-peer cloud-connect feature that lets these devices be reached without a port being opened on the firewall. The objective appears to be building attack infrastructure rather than surveillance, which is why this belongs on an enterprise IT page rather than a physical-security one. Cameras are bought by facilities, installed by an integrator, connected to the corporate network, and then never patched, never inventoried and never given a credential rotation. A compromised recorder is a foothold inside the perimeter with routes to everything else on that VLAN, and it will not appear in your endpoint console.

Medium Research — Phishing technique

Microsoft threat researchers described a large-scale phishing campaign using ASCII smuggling: invisible characters from the Unicode Tags block, U+E0000 to U+E007F, inserted inside finance-related keywords so that a word such as “funding” is split by a character the recipient never sees but a keyword-matching filter does. The forward-looking part of Microsoft’s guidance is the more interesting one — the same normalisation should be applied before email content is fed to AI assistants, because invisible characters that fool a keyword list can equally carry instructions a model will read and a human reviewer will not.
