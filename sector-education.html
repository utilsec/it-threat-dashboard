Once in, intruders have planted the WHIPSHOT web shell and SLAPSHOT tunneller, created a rogue superuser account named sec_monitor, archived and exfiltrated the appliance configuration from /flash/nsconfig and tampered with logs. Mandiant’s Charles Carmakal warns that upgrading alone neither removes that access nor undoes stolen credentials. One researcher reported signs of code execution on a patched honeypot; watchTowr and Citrix describe a crash. Precautionary shutdowns of Citrix environments at Dutch hospitals and a ministry were not confirmed compromises. High Breach

The Technical University of Denmark says attackers used compromised credentials to download data from its identity and access management system — up to 200,000 current and former staff, students, guests and partners going back to 2003, with national ID numbers for current users

The Technical University of Denmark (DTU) disclosed on 3 October 2026 that attackers used compromised credentials to get into DTUBasen, its identity and access management system, and download data. Up to 200,000 people may be affected — about 40,000 active and about 160,000 former users — covering anyone who has been an employee, student, guest or external partner since 2003. For current users the data includes Danish civil registration (CPR) numbers, names, home addresses and profile photos, work email, job title and office location, and next-of-kin names, relationships and phone numbers. For former users, DTU says home addresses, photos and next-of-kin details are deleted automatically six months after they leave, which narrows what it still held about them. DTU is notifying people through e-Boks, Denmark’s official digital mailbox; students without a CPR number are not notified directly. University Director Bjarke Bak Christensen called it a serious attack on the university. What is not known: DTU has not said whose credentials were used or how they were obtained, when the access took place, or whether MFA was in place, and no attacker or ransom demand has been reported. It is separate from the misuse of lookup access to the national CPR register that a Danish ministry disclosed on 5 October. It matters because an identity system is the one place a university keeps a record of everyone it has ever let in, and two decades of former users is the retention tail that turns a single credential compromise into a population-scale breach (editorial assessment).

Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München published a data-protection notice on Saturday 19 September 2026 describing unauthorised access to a server that stores the information students supply when they enrol. The categories LMU lists are the ones that make this more than a routine university breach: names, dates and places of birth, gender, postal addresses, phone numbers and LMU email addresses; bank account details (IBAN and account-holder names); and, for some students, health-insurance numbers, BAföG student-aid identifiers, prior educational history and in some cases the reason for a leave of absence. Enrolment deadlines have been extended for students affected by the outage. The Record reports that the intrusion was detected the preceding Wednesday and that LMU has not yet determined when the access began or how long it lasted. What is not known matters as much as what is. LMU has not said how many people are affected or how much data was involved, no attacker has been named, and no ransom demand or leak-site listing has been reported. This page does not print a student count and treats the attribution as open. The lesson is structural rather than German. An enrolment system is where a university keeps the data it needs once and then keeps forever: banking details for fee refunds and stipends, insurance numbers for statutory checks, aid identifiers and the personal circumstances behind a leave of absence. It is often an older, internally facing application with broad read access for administrative staff and a long retention tail, and a single server compromise reaches all of it at once.
