Top Story

A breach at logistics contractor CEVA ripples into half a dozen retail brands' customer data

CEVA Logistics, a major European contract-logistics provider, was compromised on 29 July 2026 in an intrusion that halted outbound shipments from eight of its warehouses; air, ocean, ground and rail transport were unaffected. CEVA notified affected retail customers on 1 August, and the scale became public the week of 10 August. The company says names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details were compromised, but that payment and credential data were not. Because CEVA runs fulfilment for a wide client roster, the exposure reached consumer-facing retailers directly: Dutch online retailer Bol, department-store chain De Bijenkorf and eyewear retailer Ace & Tate were all named, as was Pokémon Center, whose UK and Germany operations saw some customer orders cancelled as a result of the disruption. Valve confirmed Steam-related logistics were also affected. No ransomware group or extortion actor has claimed responsibility, which keeps the incident in a narrower category than a typical named-gang breach, and CEVA has not said how the intrusion began. The Dutch data-protection authority has opened an inquiry given the number of Netherlands-based retailers involved. For retail security teams the practical lesson is that a single upstream logistics or fulfilment compromise can generate breach notifications for every downstream brand that shipped through it, regardless of how well each retailer's own storefront and payment systems were defended.

Trezor customer data exposed after fulfilment partner ShipMonk falls to a Metabase zero-day

Hardware-wallet maker Trezor disclosed that its fulfilment partner ShipMonk was breached, exposing contact and order details for roughly 13,689 Trezor customers; the company says no seed phrases or private keys were involved. The breach traces back to attackers exploiting Metabase (CVE-2026-72898, CVSS 10.0) as a zero-day before any patch existed. The same flaw hit laptop maker Framework on 3 August, exposing names, emails, addresses, phone numbers and billing information for its entire customer base — underscoring that the exposure was in a shared analytics tool used across multiple e-commerce and fulfilment operations rather than a flaw specific to any one retailer.

High Incident

SafePal plugin bug retained order data far longer than intended, exposing ~39,800 customers

Crypto-wallet retailer SafePal disclosed that a bug in a plugin kept customer order records long past their intended retention period, exposing names, addresses, emails, phone numbers and order details for approximately 39,798 customers who placed orders between March 2025 and April 2026. The issue is a data-handling and retention failure rather than an active intrusion by a named actor, but the exposed fields are the same contact and order data attackers use for follow-on phishing and account-takeover attempts against e-commerce customers.

Medium Research

Two unrelated breaches this window both trace back to a retailer’s logistics or fulfilment partner

CEVA Logistics' compromise reached Bol, De Bijenkorf, Ace & Tate, Pokémon Center and Valve/Steam purely because CEVA handled their shipping. Separately, Trezor customer data was exposed not because Trezor itself was breached, but because its fulfilment partner ShipMonk ran a vulnerable Metabase instance. Neither retailer's own storefront, payment stack or internal network was the point of failure; both incidents originated one or two hops upstream, in vendors that most customers never hear of. That pattern matters more for retail than for most sectors because fulfilment and logistics outsourcing is close to universal, and a retailer's own security posture does not extend to a partner's analytics tools or warehouse IT.

Patch-to-exploit windows for major e-commerce platforms are shrinking to days — or nothing at all

Three separate platform flaws this window illustrate how little runway retail IT teams now get. SAP patched SAP Commerce Cloud's CVE-2026-58231 (CVSS 10.0) on 11 August; exploitation attempts hit honeypots within three days, a public proof-of-concept followed, and Shadowserver counted more than 4,200 internet-exposed instances still reachable. Metabase, used across many retail analytics and BI deployments, was exploited as a zero-day with no patch available at all. The pattern across all three: assume public disclosure of a patch is itself a starting gun for attackers, not an end state.

This window’s retail breaches target contact and order data, not payment cards

Across CEVA's downstream retailers, the Trezor/ShipMonk exposure and the SafePal incident, the data actually taken is strikingly consistent: names, postal addresses, phone numbers, emails and order history — not card numbers, credentials or, in Trezor's case, cryptocurrency seed phrases or keys. None of the three incidents' operators reported payment-data loss. That data set is less immediately monetizable than card numbers but is well suited to targeted phishing, SIM-swap enablement and account-takeover attempts against the same customers later, which is a slower-burn risk that doesn't show up in initial breach headlines.

Max-severity SAP Commerce Cloud flaw exploited within three days of patch — 4,200+ instances exposed

SAP patched CVE-2026-58231 (CVSS 10.0), an improper-authorization flaw in the Commerce Cloud Data Hub Adapter that allows arbitrary code execution, on 11 August 2026. Exploitation attempts against honeypots began within three days, and a public proof-of-concept has since circulated. Shadowserver counted more than 4,200 internet-exposed SAP Commerce Cloud instances. Commerce Cloud underpins storefronts, catalog and order-management for large retailers, so a working RCE against it is a direct path to customer and order data as well as backend systems.

Critical Advisory

Metabase max-severity SQL injection added to CISA KEV after zero-day exploitation drove retail-adjacent breaches

CISA added Metabase's CVE-2026-72898 (CVSS 10.0, both v3.1 and v4.0) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August 2026, with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August. The flaw is an unauthenticated SQL injection via the /reset_password endpoint that grants full admin access, affecting open-source Metabase 0.58.0–0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0–1.63.x before 1.63.5. It was exploited as a zero-day before any patch existed, and is the root cause behind both the Framework and ShipMonk/Trezor data thefts covered elsewhere on this page. Metabase is commonly used for internal analytics and BI dashboards at retailers and their vendors, often with access to order and customer data.
