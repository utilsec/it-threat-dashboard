French supermarket refrigeration attack, ten days on: no new attack nights since 13 October, but Forescout finds 12,976 refrigeration management services exposed online in 99 countries

Forescout published on 22 October a count of refrigeration management interfaces reachable from the internet: 12,976 service observations on 6,017 public IP addresses in 99 countries and territories, led by Copeland and Dixell XWEB and Danfoss StoreView, with about 80% of the StoreView services it saw on firmware older than 4.3.1. It counts services, not devices, and says the entry route, tooling and identity of the French attackers are still unknown. Those attacks, on the nights of 11–12 and 12–13 October, pushed display cases and freezers in about 700 French supermarkets (Finance Minister Roland Lescure, 13 October) into forced or extended defrost; Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché with Netto, and Monoprix confirmed being hit, the Paris prosecutor reports fires at some sites, and its cybercrime section is investigating with OFAC and ANSSI. CERT-FR’s alert CERTFR-2026-ALE-012, unchanged since its third version on 16 October, names Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC and, since 13 October, Copeland and Dixell XWEB, and still says the campaigns are “probably still ongoing”; no new attack nights have been reported since 13 October. There is no CVE, claim, attribution or arrest. Les Mousquetaires, Intermarché’s parent, says the intrusion came through a refrigeration service provider — a victim statement, not an official finding — and Danfoss says most affected units were end-of-life AK-SM 800 and AK-SC 355 models. Sekoia’s report of activity in other European countries has not been confirmed by any authority. Forescout also counts more than 20 claimed hacktivist attacks on refrigeration systems since January, mostly in Spain and Italy. For retailers this is a store-operations risk: refrigeration, HVAC and energy controllers installed and remotely managed by contractors, often left reachable from the internet so the contractor can log in.

High Campaign

A Chinese-speaking criminal pointed open-source Cairn AI agents at more than 1,200 sites, mostly online shops, reaching the websites of over 100 companies — including one very large US retailer

NorForge researcher Snorre Fagerland found more than 20 likely malicious control panels for Cairn, an open-source AI tool built for authorised penetration testing, after he began seeing them on the internet around 2 October, and Reuters reviewed the attacker’s records. Per The Hacker News on 20 October, the agents targeted more than 1,200 sites, reportedly in the US, Japan and Australia, with tasks to run code on a shop and plant a web-shell uploader, apparently to keep access to shop and payment systems for later use. Reuters, which described the operator as a Chinese-speaking criminal, says the websites of more than 100 companies were reached. NorForge puts the overall success rate at about 10%, with a successful intrusion taking a little under 23 hours on average, and it independently confirmed the compromise of one very large US retailer. Deere and SharkNinja told Reuters the damage was limited or contained, and Cummins said it was investigating a report about a vendor-run site. One documented break-in chained write access to a memcached session store, unsafe handling of stored PHP objects and old Zend Framework code. A proof of concept for the Magento StyleSmuggler flaw, CVE-2026-75650, sat on the attacker’s server, but NorForge found no evidence it was used. How much data was taken is not known. No source links this operation to the separate Cairn-based card-skimming campaign Gambit described in September (see below). Editorial assessment: Fagerland put the cost at about US$50 in AI tokens per successful break-in, so patient, automated probing of mid-size storefronts is now cheap.

Two WordPress vendor compromises in a week: stolen UpdraftCentral keys used to plant admin backdoors on sites, and Rank Math PRO served with a backdoor from its own website for 11 hours

TeamUpdraft, which makes the UpdraftPlus backup plugin (more than 4 million active installs), found unauthorised access to teamupdraft.com over the weekend of 17–18 October and believes the attacker reached its customer database, including the keys sites use to connect to UpdraftCentral, UpdraftVault and UpdraftClone; payment details and plugin downloads were not affected. From 15:32 UTC on 18 October the stolen keys were used to send signed commands that installed disguised backdoor plugins — Patchstack logged at least 99 plugin names — and created hidden administrator accounts. Nethemba says a newer variant logs in as existing administrators, so fully patched sites are exposed while the keys stay valid, and that the first fix failed to remove cloud keys. About 10% of UpdraftPlus sites hold such a key, roughly 400,000 sites potentially at risk; that is an estimate of exposure, not confirmed compromises, and no count of hit sites has been published. Separately, Rank Math PRO 3.0.123 downloaded from the vendor’s own site between 20:02 UTC on 15 October and 07:12 UTC on 16 October carried a backdoor that contacts rank1cdn[.]com, recreates itself on page visits and exposes database credentials and API keys; the free WordPress.org version was not affected, and Rank Math says “a limited number of users” were hit without giving a count. Editorial assessment: many WooCommerce stores run one or both plugins, and an administrator account on a store reaches orders, customer data and the checkout page.

Capcom confirms a breach of its CAPCOM ID account system: about 11.7 million email addresses and login records exposed, and 196 accounts hijacked

Capcom said on 22 October that an unauthorised party accessed CAPCOM ID, the shared account system for its games and services, after it detected the intrusion on 19 October. For more than 11.7 million accounts the exposed data is the email address, account creation date, last login time, login count and the IP address of the last login; 43 accounts also lost date of birth, gender, nickname and country. The attackers changed the passwords of 196 accounts, locking their owners out, and Capcom has reset them. Capcom says other members’ passwords and card data were not exposed. It reported the breach to police and to Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission on 21 October, is notifying users in stages, and says it has identified but not disclosed how the attackers got in. No group has claimed it. Editorial assessment: for any brand that sells direct, one customer-account system concentrates risk — email addresses and login history are enough for targeted phishing and credential stuffing against shoppers, and the 196 hijacked accounts show the attackers could change accounts as well as read them.

ASOS app users received an “unauthorised customer notification” in the first week of October, which The Register reported on 6 October. The lesson is the channel more than the data: the platform that sends push notifications and marketing messages reaches every customer at once, so whoever controls it can phish the whole base in the brand’s own voice.

Gambit’s AI-agent skimming campaign: 27 organisations breached, skimmers confirmed on 19 of their sites and more than 600,000 card records taken from two of them — still one vendor’s figures, with no victim named

Gambit, an AI-security company, has published an investigation, reported by BleepingComputer on 23 September and The Register on 25 September, into a financially motivated operator who used three open-source AI-agent frameworks to find, break into and skim e-commerce and booking sites. According to Gambit, Strix handled vulnerability scanning, Cairn autonomous exploitation and Hermes orchestration, with the human operator giving the agents short goal instructions; the reporting describes the attacker’s own use of the frameworks and does not describe any compromise of the model provider. The Register’s account, citing Gambit’s threat-intelligence director Eyal Sela, puts 27 companies breached “to varying degrees”, skimmers ordered against all 27 and confirmed present on 19 of their websites, and more than 600,000 card records “from just two victim companies”. Separately, more than 100 other infected sites have been identified, for at least 119 in all. The operator’s spend was roughly $12,000–18,000. Gambit also says the agents ran deletion or cleanup routines that could disrupt a victim’s operations, “and this has indeed happened in some of the breaches”, which turns a skimming incident into a data-loss one. Victims are described only by type: a Fortune 500 hospitality company, a major US airline, a private US industrial-supplies distributor and a US online fashion retailer. How the sites were first breached is not stated and no CVE is named. It led the 24 September edition and stays below confirmed incidents because the figures still come from a single vendor, with no company, regulator or card brand confirming any of them. What stays true either way is the economics: at about $25 a scan, small and mid-sized storefronts that were never worth a human operator’s time are now in range.

It is separate from the “Click2Shell” flaw that WordPress 7.1.1 fixed. The retail relevance is simply where these devices sit.
