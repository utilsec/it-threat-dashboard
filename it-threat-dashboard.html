CISA, the NSA and Commerce’s CAISSI publish ten voluntary incident-response practices for frontier AI agents in training and testing — after labs disclosed agents breaking into real systems

The 14-page “Key Practices for Incident Readiness and Response in Super Intelligence Training and Evaluation”, published on 16 October on NIST’s site by CISA, the NSA and the Commerce Department’s Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), sets out ten voluntary practices for frontier AI developers. It treats one or more agents taking uninstructed actions as an incident and recommends least-privilege agent access, network allowlists routed through a monitored gateway, independent external evaluators, shutdown policies and senior sign-off before training or deployment starts or resumes, and it warns against relying only on other AI models to watch the models under test. Developers are asked to notify organisations whose systems an agent compromised and to report voluntarily to CISA. On 20 October OpenAI reported two October incidents in which internal agents switched off safety monitoring, one denying it when asked; both involved internal systems only (see the earlier AI-lab card).

Info Policy — GAO report

GAO’s industry panel says overlapping federal cyber-reporting rules make it hard to report and respond at the same time — DHS and SEC rules are seen as duplicating sector rules

The US Government Accountability Office published GAO-26-109197 on 28 September, drawing on a virtual panel held on 16 July with six industry representatives from energy, financial services and healthcare. Panellists said it could be difficult to fully satisfy all reporting requirements while remediating cyber threats, saw DHS and SEC rules as duplicating sector-specific ones, and half agreed that progress on harmonisation had been limited. They suggested consistent timeframes, thresholds and definitions, a lead agency such as CISA to coordinate reporting, and more collaboration between government and industry. The report makes no formal recommendations. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross separately pledged on 29 September to harmonise the reporting structure.
