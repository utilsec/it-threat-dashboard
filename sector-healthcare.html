Top Story

Ransomware group hijacks AnMed’s own Facebook page to demand ransom as the four-hospital system enters a second week of disrupted appointments

AnMed, a nonprofit four-hospital health system serving upstate South Carolina and northeast Georgia, has been working to recover from a ransomware attack first detected on 26 July 2026 that knocked out core IT systems across the organization. More than a week later, ten of AnMed’s facilities remained closed to new and existing patient appointments, forcing care to be rerouted and delaying non-emergency services across the region it serves. On 11 August the extortion group calling itself “The Gentlemen” escalated the pressure campaign by gaining access to AnMed’s own Facebook page and posting ransom demands directly to the health system’s public audience — turning a hospital’s own communications channel into part of the extortion itself. AnMed regained control of the account and removed the posts. The group claims to have stolen roughly 6TB of data, including sensitive health records, but AnMed has not verified the volume or contents of any stolen data, and that figure should be treated as an unconfirmed extortion claim rather than an established fact. The health system has not disclosed whether ransomware directly encrypted clinical systems or given a recovery timeline for the facilities still closed. For patients and referring providers, the practical impact is real regardless of what is eventually confirmed about any stolen data: appointment access, not just data confidentiality, has been degraded for more than a week.

High Incident

Health-IT vendor Unlimited Technology Systems raises breach total to 3.8 million patients

Unlimited Technology Systems, an Ohio-based business associate that hosts the g4-Centricity for Vector platform for specialty healthcare practices, disclosed that a ransomware intrusion between 5 and 10 October 2025 ultimately affected roughly 3.8 million patients — a substantially higher figure than earlier estimates. The company reported the incident to HHS on 21 July 2026 and says exposed data may include Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, diagnosis details and scanned identification documents, while stating that full medical records, imaging and financial account numbers were not involved. A company spokesperson said it has found no evidence the data has been misused and that all customers whose data was believed involved have been directly notified. The case is a reminder that business-associate breaches can end up touching far more patients than any single clinic client, and that initial scope estimates in healthcare vendor breaches routinely climb as investigations continue.

Medium Incident

Philips confirms a contained breach as Cl0p’s PTC Windchill extortion campaign reaches manufacturers with medical-device engineering data

The Cl0p ransomware group’s exploitation of an unauthenticated deserialization flaw in PTC’s Windchill product-lifecycle-management platform, CVE-2026-12569 (CVSS 9.3), has produced a leak-site list of roughly 43 to 50 claimed victims, including Philips, GE and Shell. Philips has confirmed the incident, describing it as an attempted compromise of a specific enterprise server that it identified and contained, and stating the intrusion had no impact on customer environments; GE and Shell say they are still investigating the claims made against them. Cl0p claims to have taken backups, project plans, facility photos, drawings, diagrams and blueprints from the affected systems. PTC began patching Windchill in June 2026, and researchers documented attackers chaining a separate FlexPLM information-disclosure bug with a Windchill login-servlet flaw to plant web shells, with exploitation ramping up from 20 July. For healthcare, the relevance is that a major medical-device manufacturer’s engineering environment — not a hospital’s clinical network — is the confirmed touchpoint here, underscoring that PLM and design-data platforms at device makers are now squarely in ransomware crews’ sights.

Medium Research

Health-ISAC: healthcare cyberattacks rose 14% in the first half of 2026, driven by impersonation and social engineering

A Health-ISAC report published 10 August 2026 found that cyberattacks against the healthcare sector climbed 14% year over year in the first half of 2026, with the increase concentrated in impersonation and social-engineering-driven attacks rather than novel technical exploitation. Health-ISAC chief security officer Errol Weiss said hospital CISOs he talks to are taking the threat seriously, while pointing to persistent resource and staffing constraints as a primary obstacle to keeping pace. The report cites the sector’s familiar structural difficulties — legacy-device patching, medical-device complexity and always-on clinical operations that resist maintenance windows — as reasons the growth is hard to reverse quickly. Health-ISAC separately ran “Operation Vital Signs,” described as a first-of-its-kind exercise stress-testing health-sector cyber resilience. Read alongside the AnMed and Unlimited Technology Systems items on this page, the data points toward people-and-process gaps — identity verification, vendor oversight — as much as unpatched software.

Medium Intel

Akira affiliate forces Windows Safe Mode to blind EDR after breaching a SonicWall VPN with no MFA

Huntress documented an Akira ransomware affiliate breaching a network through an internet-exposed SonicWall VPN device that had no multi-factor authentication configured. About two hours after gaining VPN access, the attacker used RDP to reach the domain controller and moved laterally to an application server. From there, using AnyDesk for remote access, the attacker forced the target system into Windows Safe Mode with Networking — a limited-driver startup state that disabled both the Huntress EDR agent and Microsoft Defender’s real-time protection for roughly ten minutes. In that window the attacker archived and exfiltrated data to cloud storage, but the ransomware payload itself crashed on launch inside Safe Mode due to memory errors, and Defender quarantined it after the system rebooted normally, so this particular attack ended in data theft without encryption. The technique is notable for hospital IT teams specifically because the entry point — an unpatched or MFA-less VPN appliance — is the same class of exposure discussed elsewhere on this page for clinician and third-party remote access.

Critical Advisory

Suspected China-nexus actor mass-exploits critical VMware vCenter flaw across 361 victim networks in five days

A critical directory-traversal flaw in the VMware vCenter Server syslog service, CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8), allows unauthenticated remote code execution against the platform most large IT shops — including hospital systems — use to run their virtualization fabric. Broadcom shipped a fix on 29 July 2026, but DFIR firm QUIRSO observed the first attacker contact just five days later, on 3 August, and by 7 August had logged 361 distinct victim IP addresses across 47 countries, with Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France hit hardest. The observed chain uses the path-traversal flaw to gain a foothold, plants a malicious cron job for persistence, and then installs the open-source reverse_ssh tool for command-and-control. A suspected China-nexus operator has been seen deploying a Babuk-derived ransomware payload that researchers assess, with moderate confidence, may be a smokescreen for deeper intrusion rather than the primary objective. The flaw is not yet listed in CISA’s KEV catalog. In a hospital environment, vCenter typically manages the guest virtual machines, datastores and often the backup infrastructure behind clinical applications and the EHR, so a compromise here can reach far beyond any single system.

Critical Advisory

Metabase zero-day exploited before a patch existed, hits laptop maker Framework and a Trezor fulfilment partner

CVE-2026-72898, an unauthenticated SQL injection in the open-source Metabase analytics platform’s /reset_password endpoint, carries the maximum CVSS score of 10.0 under both v3.1 and v4.0 and grants attackers full administrative access. It was exploited as a zero-day, before any patch was available, and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August 2026 with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August. Two confirmed victims illustrate the reach: laptop maker Framework disclosed on 3 August that names, emails, addresses, phone numbers and billing information for its entire customer base were exposed, and ShipMonk, a fulfilment partner for hardware-wallet maker Trezor, confirmed roughly 13,689 Trezor customers were affected, though no seed phrases or private keys were involved. Affected versions are open-source Metabase 0.58.0 through 0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0 through 1.63.x before 1.63.5. Metabase and similar BI tools are widely used inside healthcare organizations to build analytics dashboards over claims, utilization and quality data that routinely include PHI, making an admin-level compromise of the platform a direct data-exposure risk rather than just an IT nuisance.

High Advisory

Cisco ASA and FTD VPN flaw actively exploited to crash devices used for clinician and third-party remote access

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-20349 (CVSS 8.6), a denial-of-service flaw in the Remote Access SSL VPN service of Secure Firewall ASA and FTD software, after confirming active exploitation. Insufficient error checking when processing certain HTTP requests lets an unauthenticated remote attacker force a vulnerable device to reload, and the flaw affects IKEv2 Remote Access VPN with client services, SSL-VPN and Zero Trust Network Access configurations. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August 2026, with a 14 August deadline for federal agencies. Cisco has published hotfixes across the ASA 9.16–9.24 and FTD 7.0–10.0 series but says there is no workaround for unpatched devices. ASA and FTD appliances are a common choice for the VPN concentrators hospitals and health systems use for remote clinician access and third-party vendor support, so a successful DoS against one can disrupt remote access for clinical and support staff even though this flaw does not itself grant code execution.

High Advisory

Windows zero-day used by North Korea’s Lazarus Group to elevate privileges after phishing lures

August’s Patch Tuesday fixed 398 CVEs, including one actively exploited zero-day: CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free vulnerability in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (AFD.sys), rated CVSS 7.0. It is a local elevation-of-privilege flaw, not remote code execution on its own, but Check Point ties its exploitation to North Korea’s Lazarus Group as part of Operation Dream Job: attackers use trojanised PDF viewers and DLL side-loading to first gain a foothold, then exploit the AFD.sys flaw to escalate privileges and deploy a newly identified backdoor called Troy, targeting defence and aerospace organizations in France, Germany, Brazil and India. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 11 August 2026, the same day Microsoft shipped the fix. Healthcare organizations are not the named targets of this specific campaign, but the underlying flaw is present on any unpatched Windows endpoint, including clinical and administrative workstations, so the patch itself is broadly relevant even where the espionage angle is not.

High Advisory

CISA, FBI and partners name healthcare among Gunra ransomware’s confirmed target sectors in a joint advisory

A joint cybersecurity advisory, AA26-222A, published 10 August 2026, details Gunra, a Conti-derived ransomware-as-a-service operation running double-extortion attacks against healthcare, government and other critical-infrastructure organizations. The advisory describes affiliates gaining initial access primarily through internet-facing VPN gateways and firewalls, including two previously disclosed FortiOS/FortiProxy authentication-bypass flaws, CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472, that remain unpatched in some environments more than a year after disclosure. Once inside, affiliates steal data before encrypting it and use short ransom deadlines to pressure payment. Because healthcare is explicitly named as a targeted sector in the advisory rather than merely inferred from leak-site claims, it is a direct signal that hospital and health-system edge devices are within this group’s stated targeting, not just incidental collateral. This is the same category of exposed VPN and firewall infrastructure implicated in the Cisco ASA/FTD flaw elsewhere on this page.

Kemp LoadMaster command-injection flaw draws hundreds of exploitation attempts within days of KEV listing

Progress Software’s Kemp LoadMaster application delivery controller carries CVE-2026-8037 (CVSS 9.6), an unauthenticated command-injection vulnerability. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 7 August 2026, triggering a Binding Operational Directive 26-04 deadline of 10 August for federal agencies. Researchers logged 792 exploitation attempts originating from 65 distinct IP addresses across 18 countries around the time of the KEV listing, indicating broad, automated scanning and attack activity rather than a narrow, targeted campaign. LoadMaster and similar load balancers commonly sit in front of patient-portal, EHR-access and other clinical web applications to manage traffic and availability, so an unauthenticated command-injection flaw in one is a direct path to the infrastructure that fronts those services.

High Advisory

August Patch Tuesday fixes critical unauthenticated RCE in Windows DHCP Server alongside a likely-to-be-exploited privilege-escalation flaw

Microsoft’s August 2026 Patch Tuesday, which addressed 398 CVEs in total, included CVE-2026-62823 (CVSS 8.8), a critical unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw in Windows DHCP Server triggered by crafted network packets. The same update round fixed CVE-2026-62832, a publicly disclosed privilege-escalation flaw in the Windows User Profile Service that relies on link-following; Microsoft warns that exploitation of this second flaw is likely, though neither is reported as currently under active attack. DHCP servers are foundational, always-on infrastructure in most hospital networks, handling address assignment for everything from clinical workstations to networked medical devices, which makes an unauthenticated RCE path into that service a high-value target even without confirmed in-the-wild exploitation yet.

Info Policy

Federal judge locks down handling of stolen Change Healthcare data with seven new security rules

A federal magistrate judge overseeing the combined multidistrict litigation against UnitedHealth Group, Change Healthcare, Optum and United HealthCare Services approved seven new security protocols governing how the dataset stolen in the 2024 Change Healthcare ransomware attack may be handled during the litigation. The rules, agreed by both parties before judicial approval, limit plaintiffs to a single complete copy of the data on a federally certified encrypted drive, require re-encryption to current standards with passwords transmitted separately, mandate that any computer used to process the data be fully air-gapped with wireless disabled, cap small working extracts at 25 individuals’ records behind complex 16-character-plus passwords, require detailed chain-of-custody logging visible to the defendants, set a 48-hour window to report any unauthorized access, and require destruction of all copies within 30 days after the litigation concludes. The order does not change what happened in the original 2024 breach; it governs how the already-stolen patient data may be used and secured going forward inside the litigation itself.
