Huntress saw a spike on 13 November in attacks that abused ScreenConnect’s Run Command feature to install rogue remote-access clients on customers’ machines. Many charities rely on an outside IT provider that runs ScreenConnect (editorial assessment).

On 27 October the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada opened an investigation into Navigate360, the company that provides and manages the P3 tip-management platform used by Crime Stoppers chapters across Canada. It will examine the security safeguards Navigate360 had in place at the time of the breach and whether the company complied with PIPEDA, the federal private-sector privacy law. Crime Stoppers itself is not being investigated: the regulator notes that PIPEDA generally does not apply to not-for-profits, and says it has contacted the charity to offer breach-response guidance. The effects have spread since Toronto Crime Stoppers, a charity run by a volunteer civilian board, stopped taking tips by phone, online or through its app late on 21 October. A breach notice Navigate360 filed with California’s attorney general, posted on 23 October, gives 21 November 2025 as the date of the breach. CBC has reported that more than 1.5 million tips from chapters across Canada were in data released in March and claimed by a hacktivist group. Navigate360 did not immediately respond to Global News. A charity’s promise of confidentiality is only as strong as the supplier that holds the data.

CISA now lists the Nextcloud Collabora flaw as exploited — attackers plant web shells through the built-in document server, and switching the app off does not fix it

CISA added CVE-2026-110447, a command-injection flaw in the optional Collabora Online Built-in CODE Server app for Nextcloud (richdocumentscode), to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 23 October with a federal deadline of 26 October, which has now passed; MITRE, as the CVE numbering authority, scores it CVSS v4.0 9.2. The flaw lets an unauthenticated request run commands on the server through the app’s proxy.php whenever the local document server is down — during a restart, an upgrade or a crash. According to the CVE record and reports from three Nextcloud administrators, covered by The Hacker News on 14 October, the first logged attack was on 10 September: attackers dropped one-line PHP web shells named shx*.php under apps/richdocumentscode/, sent basic system details to webhook.site and, on one server, searched for credential stores and cryptocurrency wallets. The scale is unknown and no root access has been reported. The fix, version 26.4.303 of the app, was released on 11 September (it needs Nextcloud 30 or later) but was not flagged as a security update at the time. Disabling the app does not close the hole: it has to be upgraded or removed. Standalone Collabora Online and installations using only the richdocuments connector are not affected. Nextcloud is a popular choice for NGOs that want file sharing they control, often run by a volunteer or a small hosting company (editorial assessment), and those servers hold exactly the documents the organisation chose not to put in a big-tech cloud.

Apple brings its fix for an exploited CoreGraphics flaw to older iPhones and iPads — the devices of staff in high-risk roles come first

On 26 October Apple released iOS and iPadOS 18.7.11 for the iPhone XS, iPhone XS Max, iPhone XR and the 7th-generation iPad — devices its iOS 26 and 27 releases no longer cover — bringing them the fix for CVE-2026-86950, as BleepingComputer reported on 27 October. The same day Apple shipped a wider round of updates, including iOS and iPadOS 26.7.2 and 27.2, macOS Tahoe 26.7.2 and macOS Sequoia 15.8.2. The flaw, first fixed on 28 September in iOS and iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1 and macOS Sequoia 15.8.1, is an out-of-bounds write in CoreGraphics, the system’s graphics framework: processing a maliciously crafted file may lead to arbitrary code execution. Apple says it is aware of a report that the flaw may have been exploited in an “extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals” on versions of iOS before iOS 27. CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 29 September; its enrichment scores it CVSS 8.8, and the federal deadline of 13 October has passed. Apple has not said who was targeted or by whom. Attacks of this kind are aimed at chosen people rather than the general public, which is why it sits on the nonprofit page: the people most likely to be singled out are the human-rights researchers, journalists, lawyers and diaspora organisers that many NGOs employ or support — the same people the Citizen Lab and Star Blizzard items on this page describe — and an older iPhone handed down to a staff member or volunteer is exactly the kind of device that misses updates (editorial assessment).
