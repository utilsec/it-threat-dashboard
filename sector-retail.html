Top Story

One 3PL breach at CEVA Logistics cascades into Pokémon Center, Steam and a run of European retailers

Pokémon Center began notifying UK and Germany customers on 17 August that their data was exposed through a compromise at CEVA Logistics, the contract-logistics provider that handles its European fulfilment. Names, mailing addresses, phone numbers, email addresses and order contents were involved; payment card data was not, and some orders were cancelled as a result of the disruption. The same CEVA incident also reached Valve’s Steam hardware customers in Europe. CEVA, part of the CMA CGM Group, was compromised on 29 July; eight European warehouses were affected and goods stopped shipping, with knock-on delivery delays reported at Bol, De Bijenkorf, ING and Ace & Tate. Neither the initial access vector nor an extortion group claiming the intrusion has been made public. The lesson for retail security teams is not about Pokémon Center’s or Valve’s own defences — no storefront, payment stack or corporate network belonging to those brands was the point of failure. It is that outsourced fulfilment concentrates customer order data and shipping capacity in a small number of providers, so a single intrusion produces breach notifications, cancelled orders and delivery outages across brands with no relationship to one another. Every retailer should be able to answer today which 3PLs hold its customer order data, what the contractual notification window is, and how long the business can keep shipping without them.

Medium Incident

Claimed Entra directory theft names McDonald’s, Gap, IHG and Wyndham

An actor calling itself TheHatman claims to have taken roughly 3.6 million employee-directory records from Microsoft Entra tenants, and retail and hospitality feature heavily in the victim list: McDonald’s at a claimed 1.7 million records, plus Gap, IHG and Wyndham alongside TCS, Vodafone, HCL, Kyndryl and Hexaware. Hudson Rock ties most of the named organisations to earlier infostealer infections, which points to valid credentials harvested from infected endpoints rather than any vulnerability in Azure or Entra itself. No named retailer or hotel group has confirmed a compromise, so treat the whole claim as unverified. The security point stands regardless of the numbers: employee directory data — names, job titles, reporting lines, internal contact details — is the standard precursor to help-desk impersonation against store, franchise and property support lines, where a caller who already knows the org chart is far harder to challenge.

Critical Incident

vCenter flaw drives a mass-exploitation campaign — 361 victim IPs, reverse-SSH persistence and Babuk-derived ransomware

Broadcom disclosed CVE-2026-59310 in VMSA-2026-0006 on 29 July: a directory-traversal flaw in the VMware vCenter syslog service, CVSS 9.8, with no workaround available. Exploitation moved fast. The first victims were seen connecting to attacker infrastructure on 3 August, and by 5 August roughly 95% of an eventual 361 victim IP addresses across 47 countries had already appeared, with Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France most represented. The attacker gains root on vCenter, plants a cron job for reverse-SSH persistence, and in some cases deploys ransomware built from leaked Babuk source code. CISA added the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 August with a 21 August federal deadline. Retail relevance is direct: chain virtualisation estates, store back-office servers, warehouse and distribution-centre systems commonly sit on vSphere, and root on vCenter is a route to every virtual machine underneath it.

High Research

One 3PL compromise, many retail brands: what the CEVA cascade shows about outsourced fulfilment

The CEVA Logistics intrusion was a single compromise at a single contract-logistics provider, but the consequences landed on its retail customers. Pokémon Center notified UK and Germany shoppers of exposed contact and order data and cancelled some orders; Valve’s Steam hardware customers in Europe were caught in the same incident; and delivery delays were reported at Bol, De Bijenkorf, ING and Ace & Tate after eight European warehouses stopped shipping. None of those brands had their own storefront, payment stack or corporate network breached. For retail this pattern is structural rather than unlucky: fulfilment, returns and last-mile delivery are outsourced almost universally, a handful of providers serve very large portions of the market, and a retailer’s own controls stop at the 3PL’s warehouse IT, analytics tooling and order-data stores. The practical framing is that your customer-data blast radius is the union of every processor that touches an order, and your shipping capacity has the same concentration risk.

High Research

E-commerce platform patch-to-exploit windows are now measured in days

Two storefront platform flaws in the same week show how little runway retail IT teams get. SAP patched SAP Commerce Cloud’s CVE-2026-58231 (CVSS 10.0) on 11 August; exploitation attempts were reported on 14 August and a public proof-of-concept had circulated by 15 August — three days from fix to live attacks. Adobe’s 12 August Commerce and Magento update addressed CVE-2026-71362, and Sansec reported its WAF was already blocking exploitation attempts while Adobe was still stating it had no reports of in-the-wild exploitation. Two conclusions follow. First, publication of an e-commerce platform patch is a starting gun, so releases for the systems that run checkout belong in an emergency-change process rather than a monthly maintenance window. Second, “the vendor reports no known exploitation” is a lagging indicator and should not be used to justify deferring a storefront patch.

High Research

CISA and FBI refresh the Medusa advisory: 500+ victims and roughly 24-hour weaponisation of new flaws

CISA and the FBI updated #StopRansomware advisory AA25-071A on 18 August. The cumulative victim count passed 500 critical-infrastructure organisations as of April 2026, up from about 300 in March 2025 — roughly 200 new victims in a year. The finding that should change patch planning is the tempo: Medusa weaponises newly disclosed vulnerabilities within about 24 hours, and has been observed using exploits up to a week before public disclosure, because the group buys exploits rather than developing them. Named target sectors include IT, financial services, government facilities, healthcare and critical manufacturing. Retailers were not called out by name, but they run the same internet-facing estate Medusa favours — remote access, file transfer and virtualisation — and a 24-hour weaponisation window makes a 30-day patch SLA for perimeter systems indefensible.

Detection and hardening guidance, not a single patch Action: Pull the AA25-071A indicators and detection guidance into active hunts, and compress the emergency patch SLA for internet-facing systems to under 24 hours from vendor release rather than the standard critical window.

16 typosquatted RubyGems packages ship infostealers to developer machines

Researchers identified 16 malicious RubyGems packages published under names close to legitimate ones. Once installed, they steal browser credential stores and cryptocurrency wallet data from the machine that pulled them. The retail angle is the dependency chain: a large share of storefront, checkout and back-office tooling is built on Ruby and Rails, and the credential stores on a developer workstation or a shared build runner routinely hold live sessions for the commerce platform admin, the payment gateway dashboard and the cloud console. One mistyped gem name in a Gemfile therefore reaches production access without ever touching a production system.

Max-severity SAP Commerce Cloud flaw exploited three days after the patch, with a public PoC

SAP patched CVE-2026-58231 in SAP Commerce Cloud on 11 August 2026. Rated CVSS 10.0, the flaw combines insufficient authorisation checks with insufficient input validation, allowing an attacker to execute arbitrary code against internal components of the commerce platform. Exploitation attempts were reported on 14 August, and a public proof-of-concept was circulating by 15 August — three days from patch to live attacks, and four to a working exploit anyone can run. Commerce Cloud underpins storefronts, product catalogues and order management for large retailers, so code execution against it is a direct route to order and customer data and to the back-end systems sitting behind checkout. Any retailer running it should assume scanning is already under way and treat unpatched instances as presumed-compromised until log review says otherwise.

Critical Advisory

Adobe Commerce / Magento flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker take over a shopper’s session — no account, no interaction

CVE-2026-71362, fixed in Adobe’s 12 August Commerce and Magento update, allows an unauthenticated attacker to switch a customer session onto another customer’s account. No account of their own, no administrative rights and no user interaction from the victim are required, which removes every mitigating factor a merchant would normally rely on to buy time. Adobe stated it had no reports of exploitation in the wild; e-commerce security firm Sansec said its WAF was already blocking exploitation attempts against merchants. Storefront operators should treat the August update as urgent on the strength of that second data point. A hijacked session exposes saved addresses, order history and stored payment tokens, and can be used to place orders as the victim — damage that surfaces as customer fraud complaints rather than as a security alert.

CISA adds four actively exploited flaws to KEV with a 21 August deadline

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 August, with a 21 August remediation deadline for federal agencies: CVE-2026-33824, a double-free in the Microsoft Windows IKE service extensions (IKEEXT) leading to remote code execution; CVE-2026-59310, path traversal in Broadcom VMware vCenter leading to code execution; CVE-2026-55040, a weak-authentication security-feature bypass in Microsoft SharePoint; and CVE-2026-65400, improper authentication in Apple macOS Screen Sharing. A typical retail estate runs all four — Windows domain and VPN infrastructure at head office, vSphere behind store and distribution-centre back-office systems, SharePoint for merchandising and supplier collaboration, and macOS across design, marketing and merchandising teams. The federal deadline is a reasonable private-sector target given all four are confirmed under exploitation.

High Advisory

Windows Task Host privilege-escalation flaw now being abused by ransomware crews

CISA updated its KEV entry for CVE-2025-60710 on 18 August to record that ransomware groups are now abusing the flaw. It is a link-following privilege-escalation issue in the Windows Task Host affecting Windows 11 and Windows Server 2025, taking a low-privileged local user to SYSTEM. Microsoft patched it in November 2025 and it entered the KEV catalog on 13 April 2026, so this is a hunt-for-stragglers signal rather than a new patch cycle. That is precisely why it matters in retail: the machines that miss routine patch rings are store back-office PCs, kiosk and POS-adjacent Windows hosts, warehouse terminals and long-lived servers that nobody wants to reboot during trading hours. Those are also the hosts an intruder reaches first after a store-level foothold.

Critical Advisory

Metabase max-severity SQL injection remains KEV-listed after zero-day exploitation hit retail-adjacent vendors

CISA added Metabase’s CVE-2026-72898 (CVSS 10.0) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August 2026 with a 14 August federal deadline. The flaw is an unauthenticated SQL injection reachable via the /reset_password endpoint that grants full administrative access, affecting open-source Metabase 0.58.0–0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0–1.63.x before 1.63.5. It was exploited as a zero-day before any patch existed and was the root cause behind confirmed data thefts at laptop maker Framework and at fulfilment provider ShipMonk, the latter exposing contact and order records for Trezor customers. Metabase is widely used for internal analytics and BI at retailers and, more importantly, at the fulfilment and logistics vendors they hand order data to — which is exactly where it caused damage.

Info Policy

A processor-side breach still lands on the retailer: notification duties after the CEVA compromise

The CEVA Logistics incident produced customer notifications from Pokémon Center and reached Valve’s European Steam hardware buyers, even though the intrusion happened entirely inside a logistics contractor’s environment. That is the ordinary operation of EU and UK data-protection law rather than an anomaly: where a 3PL processes personal data on a retailer’s behalf, the retailer remains the controller and carries the notification obligation to regulators and to affected customers, on the regulator’s clock rather than the contractor’s. Retailers with European customers should confirm that their data-processing agreements set a notification window short enough to leave time to meet their own deadline, and that they can identify which customers shipped through a given provider in a given period without waiting on the provider to tell them.

Not a technical or patch issue Action: Review data-processing agreements with logistics, fulfilment and returns vendors for breach-notification timelines, and confirm you can independently determine which customers and orders a given processor handled during any window.
