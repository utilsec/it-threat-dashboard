Top Story

VMware vCenter path-traversal flaw exploited within five days of Broadcom’s patch — 361 victims counted

CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8) is a critical directory-traversal flaw in vCenter Server’s syslog service that gives an unauthenticated attacker remote code execution on the virtualization management plane. Broadcom shipped fixed builds on 29 July 2026. German incident-response firm QUIRSO recorded first contact with attacker infrastructure on 3 August — five days after the patch shipped — and by 7 August had counted 361 distinct victim IP addresses across 47 countries, more than half concentrated in Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France. The observed chain runs path traversal into a malicious cron job for persistence, then the open-source reverse_ssh tool for command-and-control back to attacker infrastructure. A suspected China-nexus operator is deploying a Babuk-derived ransomware variant that researchers believe may be a smokescreen for deeper, quieter intrusion rather than the actual objective; the attribution is assessed with moderate confidence, not confirmed. vCenter sits at the root of most private-cloud, hosting and managed-service estates — compromise there can reach every guest VM, datastore and often backup target it manages. Fixed builds are vCenter 9.1.0.0300, 9.0.2.0100, and the corresponding 8.0 U3/U2 builds; the flaw is not yet listed in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. The five-day gap between patch and mass exploitation here is the fastest of several similar races this run — SAP Commerce Cloud, Adobe Commerce, GeoServer and SharePoint all followed the same pattern — evidence that defenders now have a shrinking window to apply vendor fixes before opportunistic and organized actors alike weaponize them.

Critical Incident

Worm hijacks the npm “keyv” package and poisons hundreds more within 30 minutes

A worm compromised the widely used npm package keyv using stolen publishing credentials, then poisoned hundreds of additional packages within roughly 30 minutes of gaining its foothold. It harvests developer credentials, CI/CD tokens and cloud keys from any machine that installs an affected version, and in some cases plants hooks in editor and task-runner configuration files so the payload re-triggers on later builds. The self-propagating design mirrors recent npm supply-chain worms: stolen tokens are used to publish malicious versions into further packages the compromised account can reach, expanding the blast radius automatically rather than through one coordinated push. Because keyv and its dependents sit deep in caching and session-handling code used across countless Node.js services, the exposure reaches build pipelines and production systems well beyond any organization that depends on it directly.

Active — self-propagating, poisoned hundreds of packages within ~30 minutes Open-source supply chain — no CVE
Remove affected keyv-family versions; no single vendor patch Action: Pin and re-resolve lockfiles against known-good keyv versions, treat any host or CI/CD runner that installed an affected version as compromised, rotate npm/GitHub/cloud credentials and CI tokens, and audit repos for unauthorized commits or injected editor/task-runner hooks.
Suggested SLA: Dependency sweep & secret rotation — 24h Owner:

Critical Incident

Metabase zero-day exploited before a patch existed — Framework and Trezor’s fulfilment partner confirm breaches

CVE-2026-72898 (CVSS 10.0) is an unauthenticated SQL injection in Metabase’s password-reset endpoint that grants administrator access, affecting open-source 0.58.0–0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0–1.63.x before 1.63.5. It was exploited as a zero-day, before a fix existed. Laptop maker Framework disclosed on 3 August that names, emails, addresses, phone numbers and billing information for its entire customer base were exposed. Fulfilment provider ShipMonk, which handles logistics for hardware-wallet maker Trezor, confirmed a related breach affecting roughly 13,689 Trezor customers — no seed phrases or private keys were exposed. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August with a 14 August federal remediation deadline.

High Incident

Guest-access and public-portal abuse quietly drains customer data from Salesforce, ServiceNow and Power Pages

Two separate campaigns are exploiting loose default access on major SaaS platforms rather than any software vulnerability. A financially motivated actor SaaS-security firm Reco calls “City-Forum” has spent over a year abusing guest-user access on Salesforce Experience Cloud sites and the ServiceNow Service Portal — enumerating Salesforce Aura endpoints and an undocumented Lightning Web Runtime GraphQL interface, and querying ServiceNow’s portal search endpoint — to enumerate and steal customer records from telecoms, banks and other firms. Separately, a group tracked as ExfilSquad is crawling for Microsoft Power Pages portals left with public read access to their underlying Dataverse data; it has confirmed access at several organizations, including distributor Wesco, which disputes the claimed scale of 2.6 million exposed records. Neither Salesforce, Microsoft nor ServiceNow’s own platforms were compromised — the exposure sits entirely in customer tenant configuration.

Active — City-Forum infrastructure has run for over a year Tenant misconfiguration — no CVE
No patch — configuration hardening required Action: Audit guest-user profiles and sharing rules on every Experience Cloud site and ServiceNow portal, disable guest API/GraphQL access where unneeded, review Power Pages table permissions for public read exposure, and check access logs for high-volume anonymous querying.

Analysis traces most of a 2,500-org secrets leak to Trivy, not the LiteLLM packages first blamed

Follow-up analysis found that most of the roughly 2,500 organizations whose secrets leaked in a recent supply-chain campaign were exposed through a March compromise of Aqua Security’s Trivy scanner and its Docker images — not the later-poisoned LiteLLM packages that drew initial attention when the leak surfaced. Trivy is widely embedded in CI/CD pipelines and container-build workflows across the software industry, so the real exposure window and blast radius were larger, and older, than first reported.

High Research

Akira affiliate forces Windows Safe Mode to strip EDR protection mid-intrusion

Huntress documented an Akira ransomware affiliate breaching a network through a SonicWall VPN that lacked multi-factor authentication, then forcing an affected Windows host into Safe Mode with Networking to disable endpoint detection and antivirus tooling for roughly ten minutes — a window most EDR agents do not load into by design. The actor used that window to move and stage data before the host rebooted back to normal mode. In this case the intrusion stole data but failed to encrypt it, but the Safe Mode technique itself is notable tradecraft: it defeats a broad class of endpoint protection without requiring a driver-level bypass or vulnerability, and it is repeatable against any EDR product that does not explicitly guard against a Safe Mode boot.

Active — observed in a live Akira intrusion Tradecraft technique — exploits an unprotected VPN account, no CVE
No patch — requires EDR configuration and VPN hardening Action: Enforce MFA on all VPN/remote-access accounts including SonicWall, confirm your EDR agent is configured to load in Safe Mode with Networking (or block Safe Mode boots via policy), and alert on unexpected reboots into Safe Mode from monitored endpoints.
Suggested SLA: EDR Safe Mode hardening & VPN MFA audit — 14 days Owner:

Critical Advisory

Two commerce platforms hit within days of their patches — SAP Commerce Cloud and Adobe Commerce

SAP patched CVE-2026-58231 (CVSS 10.0) on 11 August — an improper-authorization flaw in Commerce Cloud’s Data Hub Adapter allowing arbitrary code execution — and exploitation attempts hit honeypots within three days, with a public proof-of-concept following shortly after. Shadowserver counted over 4,200 internet-exposed instances. Almost as fast, Adobe’s August patch for Commerce/Magento closed CVE-2026-71362 (CVSS 9.1), a critical authorization flaw; attackers began hijacking customer sessions almost immediately, prompting Sansec to deploy emergency WAF rules for customers still patching. Both are direct hits on the e-commerce backbone that technology vendors and SaaS platforms build storefronts on, and both show the same pattern: public disclosure of a patch is now treated by attackers as the starting gun, not a grace period.

GeoServer patches an unauthenticated SQL injection exploited within hours of disclosure

GeoServer shipped releases 3.0.1, 2.28.5 and 2.27.6 on 14 August fixing an unauthenticated SQL injection in the jsonArrayContains filter against PostGIS layers, tracked under advisory GHSA-mqjf-5f49-2fjh. No CVE has been assigned yet, and the project itself publishes no CVSS score — treat any “CVSS 9.8” figure circulating elsewhere as unverified. The project rates the issue “High.” Exploitation attempts began within hours of the 12 August disclosure, ahead of the patch landing two days later. GeoServer is embedded in mapping and geospatial infrastructure used across government, agriculture, telecom and transit environments, and instances are frequently internet-exposed by default in some deployment patterns.

High Advisory

“ShieldBreak” Defender bypass disclosed without vendor notice — no patch yet

A researcher publicly disclosed, without first notifying Microsoft, a privilege-escalation flaw in the Malware Protection Engine that bypasses an earlier Defender fix — tracked as CVE-2026-69414 and nicknamed “ShieldBreak.” The proof-of-concept is reported to work reliably against Windows 11 and Windows Server 2025. Microsoft acknowledged the issue roughly three days after disclosure but has not yet shipped a fix. Because it undermines the endpoint protection most Windows environments rely on by default, organizations cannot currently close this gap through Microsoft alone and need compensating controls while a patch is pending.

CISA and FBI issue joint advisory on Gunra ransomware-as-a-service targeting internet-facing VPN gateways

CISA and the FBI published joint advisory AA26-222A on 10 August detailing the Gunra ransomware-as-a-service operation, a Conti-derived group running double-extortion attacks against healthcare, government and critical-infrastructure targets. The advisory documents Gunra gaining initial access through internet-facing VPN gateways and firewalls, including previously known Fortinet flaws CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472, rather than through any new vulnerability. For technology, hosting and managed-service providers the advisory functions as a policy signal: the same edge devices central to remote-access and hybrid-cloud architectures remain the preferred entry point for a well-resourced ransomware operation, and the fix is the same unglamorous hygiene — patch, MFA, and edge-device inventory — the agencies have recommended for years.
