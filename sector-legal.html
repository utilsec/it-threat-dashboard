Attackers are using ConnectWise ScreenConnect at managed service providers to push rogue remote-access clients into dozens of customer organisations — the route into the many small and mid-sized firms that outsource their IT

Huntress saw a spike on 13 November in attacks on ScreenConnect partner instances, in which attackers abused the product’s Run Command feature to install rogue ScreenConnect clients on customers’ machines. Counts differ by outlet and are still rising: The Hacker News reports 205 hosts at 41 organisations, BleepingComputer six MSPs, 286 endpoints and 47 organisations. This is misuse of a legitimate feature, not a confirmed ScreenConnect vulnerability; the suspected way in is an authentication flaw in a third-party RMM+ extension (versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32, no CVE). ConnectWise has disabled five extensions — Labtech Plus, RMM Plus, RMM+ and two Password Link variants — across about 400 partner environments. The attackers persist through a fake “Windows Update Agent” service, UpdateConnect.ClientService.exe. A worm-like toolkit, SCKit, can also abuse ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9, fixed in 26.6.5), but there is no evidence it was used in this campaign. The activity is unattributed, and The Hacker News does not link it to Storm-0940, whose October abuse of ConnectWise Cloud Backup is covered under Threat Intelligence. No law or professional-services firm has been named among the victims. This leads over the exploited vCenter zero-day and the Apache Axis attacks on Confluence (both in Critical Advisories) because it is a confirmed, still-growing intrusion campaign with a direct path into firms whose desktops are managed by an MSP: one remote-access console reaches every client machine at once (editorial assessment).
