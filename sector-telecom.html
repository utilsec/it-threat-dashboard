Top Story

House probe: Chinese carriers kept U.S. network footholds as Salt Typhoon cleanup drags on

A nearly 50-page bipartisan House Select Committee on the CCP report (4 Aug) concluded that three Chinese state-owned carriers — China Telecom, China Mobile and China Unicom — retained equipment, data-center space and network interconnections in the U.S. despite the FCC revoking their authorizations, ties investigators say could give Beijing visibility into or the ability to reroute sensitive traffic. Analyzing routing data, the panel found China Mobile International’s network appeared in routes to Salt Typhoon-linked servers, while cautioning the overlap is not proof of direct involvement. It underscores that carriers breached by Salt Typhoon may never fully evict the intruders.

KDDI and five other Japanese ISPs disclose breach exposing ~14M credentials

High Advisory

Cisco Secure FMC static-credential zero-day exploited in the wild

CVE-2026-20316 is a static/default-credential flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center that lets an attacker log in as a low-privilege account and read sensitive data; Cisco warns it can be chained with other FMC bugs to escalate. Cisco confirmed in-the-wild exploitation and CISA added it to the KEV catalog (29 Jul), with a federal remediation deadline. FMC governs firewall policy across carrier and enterprise networks, so a compromise is high-leverage.

High Advisory

N-able N-central authentication-bypass flaw exploited to take over RMM servers

CVE-2026-18577 (CVSS 8.2) is an authentication-bypass and account-takeover flaw in N-able’s N-central remote monitoring & management platform — an incomplete fix for the earlier CVE-2026-18556. Attackers with admin access can abuse the built-in Take Control feature to pivot into managed endpoints, a serious risk for MSPs and network operators running distributed estates. CISA added it to the KEV catalog (3 Aug); fixed in 2026.3 HF1.

How to read the indicators. Methodology. Compiled on-demand by searching authoritative security sources including CISA advisories and the KEV catalog, and reporting from BleepingComputer, the SANS Internet Storm Center, Dark Reading, The Register, The Hacker News and SecurityWeek, plus vendor and product advisories (Microsoft, Oracle, Cisco, Citrix, Progress, SimpleHelp, Fortinet, N-able) and independent threat research. Default lookback: 7–30 days. Severity tiers are editorial judgments based on described impact; consult the linked source for official CVSS scores and affected versions. “Claimed” / “reported” items are unverified assertions; “confirmed” items have vendor or agency acknowledgment.
