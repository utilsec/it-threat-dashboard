Comparitech’s mid-year analysis found ransomware attacks against K-12 schools fell 26% worldwide in the first half of 2026 compared to the second half of 2025, while attacks on higher-education institutions rose more than 8% over the same period. Read together, the story is less “schools are safe” than “risk is redistributing”: K-12 districts appear to be catching up on basic hygiene, while universities’ larger and more decentralized attack surface keeps drawing ransomware crews.

Critical Incident

VMware vCenter RCE under mass exploitation — cross-sector, but it lands squarely on university data centers

CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8), a critical directory-traversal flaw in the vCenter Server syslog service, gives unauthenticated remote code execution. Broadcom patched it 29 Jul; DFIR firm QUIRSO saw first contact with attacker infrastructure on 3 Aug and had counted 361 distinct victim IPs across 47 countries by 7 Aug, over half in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France. The observed chain is path traversal, then a malicious cron job for persistence, then the open-source reverse_ssh framework for command and control; a suspected China-nexus operator is also deploying a Babuk-derived ransomware variant that researchers believe is a smokescreen for deeper intrusion, though attribution is only moderate confidence. This is a cross-sector campaign, not an education-specific attack — but universities and colleges run vCenter to manage the virtualization clusters behind research computing, ERP and email, so a single exposed instance can hand an attacker guest VMs, datastores and often backups at once.

High Incident

Akira affiliate disables EDR via an unprotected SonicWall VPN and Windows Safe Mode

Huntress documented an Akira ransomware affiliate breaching a network through a SonicWall VPN that had no MFA enabled, then forcing the endpoint into Windows Safe Mode with Networking to disable EDR and antivirus for roughly ten minutes before stealing data; encryption ultimately failed. This is a single confirmed intrusion at an organization Huntress has not named, not an education-specific attack, but it is exactly the kind of gap campus IT teams should treat as live risk: VPN concentrators are frequently deployed at colleges and universities without MFA because of budget or legacy-system constraints.

Confirmed — one documented intrusion (technique, not a mass campaign) SonicWall VPN without MFA + Safe Mode EDR bypass
No single patch — missing MFA is the entry point Action: Require MFA on every SonicWall or other VPN concentrator serving the campus, and add detections for unscheduled reboots into Safe Mode with Networking.

High Research

Self-propagating npm worm poisons hundreds of packages via the keyv library

A worm distributed through the widely used npm package keyv used stolen publishing credentials to poison hundreds of further packages within roughly 30 minutes, harvesting developer credentials, CI/CD tokens and cloud keys, and in some cases planting editor or task-runner hooks capable of triggering the payload later. This is a cross-sector open-source supply-chain event, not education-specific, but it lands directly on university CS departments and research-computing groups that pull npm dependencies into coursework, labs and grant-funded software with the same trust assumptions as any commercial shop.

Active — hundreds of packages poisoned within ~30 minutes Supply-chain worm, not a single CVE
Remediation is dependency hygiene, not a patch Action: Audit CI/CD pipelines and lab machines for recently pulled npm packages, rotate any credentials or tokens that may have touched affected builds, and pin dependency versions in research-computing environments.

Critical Advisory

Metabase CVSS 10 flaw added to CISA KEV — a direct hit on the analytics tool behind institutional dashboards

An unauthenticated SQL injection in Metabase’s /reset_password endpoint, CVSS 10.0 under both v3.1 and v4.0, grants admin access and was exploited as a zero-day before a patch existed. CISA added it to KEV on 11 Aug with a federal remediation deadline of 14 Aug. Confirmed victims include laptop maker Framework and ShipMonk, Trezor’s fulfilment partner (roughly 13,689 customers affected). Affects open-source 0.58.0–0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0–1.63.x before 1.63.5. Cross-sector, but concretely relevant here: Metabase is a common choice for institutional-research and enrollment dashboards built on student-information-system data, so an exposed instance risks exactly the kind of records FERPA governs.

Windows zero-day exploited by Lazarus Group in Operation Dream Job, added to CISA KEV

CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (AFD.sys), CVSS 7.0, is a local elevation-of-privilege flaw patched in the August 2026 Patch Tuesday (398 CVEs total, and the only one under active exploitation) — it is local privilege escalation, not unauthenticated remote code execution. Cross-sector, not education-specific, but it patches essentially every Windows endpoint on campus, and a nation-state actor chaining it after phishing is relevant to any university with defense-research-adjacent programs.

CISA releases new K-12 cybersecurity guidance, citing 34 sector ransomware attacks and the Alamo Heights ISD breach

CISA published two free guides on 14 Aug aimed at school and district leaders — one on building a foundational cybersecurity program, the other on sustaining it — covering credential protection, device security, tested backups, incident-response planning and adoption of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. The agency cited 34 ransomware attacks against K-12 and higher-education organizations in the first half of 2026 and pointed to March’s breach at Alamo Heights ISD in Texas, which affected 26,629 people, as an example of the stakes. The guidance arrives as the Multi-State ISAC, long a source of free threat intelligence for schools, has lost roughly 70% of its membership following federal funding cuts — part of why CISA is now publishing direct, self-serve guidance rather than relying solely on that channel. Education-specific, not cross-sector.
