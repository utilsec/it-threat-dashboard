Top Story

Adobe Commerce “PolyShell” flaw CVE-2026-48356 enters CISA KEV as Sansec traces a scanner-evading Magento loader to 953 stores — including a fully patched store that was reinfected an hour after a clean scan

CISA added CVE-2026-48356 — PolyShell, an unrestricted file-upload flaw in Adobe Commerce and Magento Open Source — to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 27 October, with a federal deadline of 10 November. Adobe rates it 9.3 on CVSS v3.1 and fixed it on 14 July in bulletin APSB26-73. Sansec’s research of 24 October shows what sits behind the listing: a loader campaign that has reached 953 stores, mostly Magento, since July 2025, with the loader kept in the store database’s cms_block table rather than in theme or configuration files. In one wave on 30 March, Sansec says, the operator uploaded web shells through PolyShell and planted the loader on 471 stores within an hour; of 169 infected Magento 2 stores that were missing at least one core patch, 102 also showed file uploads through PolyShell or SessionReaper (CVE-2025-54236). A rewrite on 23 October added browser fingerprinting, so automated scanners get an empty response while real visitors get the script. The same day a store on 2.4.7-p10 with no missing core patches received the newest loader about an hour after a clean scan; Sansec had found a rogue administrator account and a GSocket backdoor on it in September, and says the October changes used no new vulnerability. Sansec has not captured the final payload, so the campaign’s purpose is not known. It leads this edition because it is a KEV-listed flaw in one of the most widely used e-commerce platforms, with a live campaign that patching alone does not stop: a store that installs APSB26-73 but keeps an attacker’s admin account or database access stays infected.

Its newsroom shows no cyber statement after 19 October, so third-quarter results on 4 November are the next likely disclosure. It no longer leads because services are largely back and nothing new has come from the company, but the lesson stands: when ordering, e-commerce and logistics fail together, recovery takes weeks, not days.

Critical Campaign

French supermarket refrigeration attack, two weeks on: no new attack nights since 13 October, but Forescout finds 12,976 refrigeration management services exposed online in 99 countries

Forescout published on 22 October a count of refrigeration management interfaces reachable from the internet: 12,976 service observations on 6,017 public IP addresses in 99 countries and territories, led by Copeland and Dixell XWEB and Danfoss StoreView, with about 80% of the StoreView services it saw on firmware older than 4.3.1. It counts services, not devices, and says the entry route, tooling and identity of the French attackers are still unknown. Those attacks, on the nights of 11–12 and 12–13 October, pushed display cases and freezers in about 700 French supermarkets (Finance Minister Roland Lescure, 13 October) into forced or extended defrost; Carrefour, Coopérative U, E.Leclerc, Intermarché with Netto, and Monoprix confirmed being hit, the Paris prosecutor reports fires at some sites, and its cybercrime section is investigating with OFAC and ANSSI. CERT-FR’s alert CERTFR-2026-ALE-012, still at its third version of 16 October as of 28 October, names Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC and, since 13 October, Copeland and Dixell XWEB, and still says the campaigns are “probably still ongoing”; no new attack nights have been reported since 13 October. There is no CVE, claim, attribution or arrest. Les Mousquetaires, Intermarché’s parent, says the intrusion came through a refrigeration service provider — a victim statement, not an official finding — and Danfoss says most affected units were end-of-life AK-SM 800 and AK-SC 355 models. Sekoia’s report of activity in other European countries has not been confirmed by any authority. Forescout also counts more than 20 claimed hacktivist attacks on refrigeration systems since January, mostly in Spain and Italy. For retailers this is a store-operations risk: refrigeration, HVAC and energy controllers installed and remotely managed by contractors, often left reachable from the internet so the contractor can log in.

A Chinese-speaking criminal pointed open-source Cairn AI agents at more than 1,200 sites, mostly online shops, reaching the websites of over 100 companies — including one very large US retailer

NorForge researcher Snorre Fagerland found more than 20 likely malicious control panels for Cairn, an open-source AI tool built for authorised penetration testing, after he began seeing them on the internet around 2 October, and Reuters reviewed the attacker’s records. Per The Hacker News on 20 October, the agents targeted more than 1,200 sites, reportedly in the US, Japan and Australia, with tasks to run code on a shop and plant a web-shell uploader, apparently to keep access to shop and payment systems for later use. Reuters, which described the operator as a Chinese-speaking criminal, says the websites of more than 100 companies were reached. NorForge puts the overall success rate at about 10%, with a successful intrusion taking a little under 23 hours on average, and it independently confirmed the compromise of one very large US retailer. Deere and SharkNinja told Reuters the damage was limited or contained, and Cummins said it was investigating a report about a vendor-run site. One documented break-in chained write access to a memcached session store, unsafe handling of stored PHP objects and old Zend Framework code. A proof of concept for the Magento StyleSmuggler flaw, CVE-2026-75650, sat on the attacker’s server, but NorForge found no evidence it was used. How much data was taken is not known. No source links this operation to the separate Cairn-based card-skimming campaign that Gambit described in September. Editorial assessment: Fagerland put the cost at about US$50 in AI tokens per successful break-in, so patient, automated probing of mid-size storefronts is now cheap.

Two WordPress vendor compromises in a week: stolen UpdraftCentral keys used to plant admin backdoors on sites, and Rank Math PRO served with a backdoor from its own website for 11 hours

TeamUpdraft, which makes the UpdraftPlus backup plugin (more than 4 million active installs), found unauthorised access to teamupdraft.com over the weekend of 17–18 October and believes the attacker reached its customer database, including the keys sites use to connect to UpdraftCentral, UpdraftVault and UpdraftClone; payment details and plugin downloads were not affected. Editorial assessment: many WooCommerce stores run one or both plugins, and an administrator account on a store reaches orders, customer data and the checkout page.

High Breach

Times Car breach: Park24’s forensic report confirms data on 6,628,007 accounts was accessed and sent out, including identity-document images for about 1.46 million, and books a ¥5.5 billion loss

Park24, the Japanese parking and mobility group, published the results of its external forensic investigation into the Times Car breach on 23 October. The attacker exploited a vulnerability in the car-sharing service’s web system, searched inside it and reached the database and file storage; the company is withholding the method to avoid further harm. For 6,628,007 Times Car accounts, access and external transmission are confirmed, covering name, address, date of birth, phone number, email address and driver’s licence details. Encrypted identity-document images, including driver’s licences, were sent out for 1,464,800 accounts and accessed without confirmed transmission for another 891,602, or 2,356,402 in all. The separate Times Club membership was also hit: 19,510 Times Club card numbers, 135,436 linked-service IDs and the last four card digits for 28 accounts; Park24 does not hold full card numbers or security codes. It has found nothing to suggest the data has been sold or published. Customers whose ID images were transmitted will receive ¥3,000, every retained ID image will be deleted by the end of October, and a committee led by outside directors and experts will review governance. In a separate notice the same day, Park24 booked a ¥5.5 billion extraordinary loss in the quarter to 31 October and cut its full-year forecast for profit attributable to owners by 12.5%, to ¥38.5 billion. The intrusion was detected on 25 September and blocked the next day. Editorial assessment: the cost here is driven by identity documents kept after verification, and any retailer that photographs licences for rental, age-restricted delivery, returns-fraud checks or account recovery holds the same liability.

Keystone hardware-wallet maker: an intruder on its website took the key its devices use to prove they are genuine, plus 25,964 buyers’ order records — 18,835 unsold wallets will be destroyed

Keystone said on 26 October that an attacker exploited a flaw in a third-party data-monitoring tool on its website, escalated privileges and took the key its hardware wallets use to prove they are genuine, along with customer data; it found and blocked the access on 20 October and first disclosed it on 24 October. Editorial assessment: as with Ledger’s reseller implants this month (see below), a security product’s sales channel is part of its security; a third-party tool on the website became the route to the company’s most sensitive key, and a buyer list for crypto wallets is a ready-made phishing target.

South Africa’s Foschini Group told the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 13 October that its UK division, TFG London, had suffered a cyber incident it became aware of on 11 October, The Record reports.
