Top Story

RingCentral confirms breach of roughly 1.6 million accounts after ShinyHunters publishes 280GB of data

RingCentral, a cloud communications and unified-collaboration platform widely used by enterprises and contact centers, has confirmed a data breach traced to a social-engineering campaign against employees or third parties with account-system access. The extortion group ShinyHunters claimed responsibility and, after RingCentral declined to pay, published an archive described as roughly 280GB covering approximately 1.6 million customer accounts. The exposed data reportedly includes names, email addresses, phone numbers and physical addresses — enough for highly targeted phishing and impersonation against both RingCentral's business customers and their end users. RingCentral has confirmed the breach occurred but has not detailed the initial access vector beyond “social engineering,” and it remains unclear whether authentication credentials, call metadata or billing records were included in the published set. For a provider that routes business voice, text and video traffic for large customer bases, an account-level breach at this scale raises the risk of vendor-impersonation and vishing campaigns that trade on the legitimacy of a known communications-platform relationship. RingCentral says it is notifying affected customers and regulators as required; ShinyHunters' publication of the full archive removes any further extortion leverage but leaves the data permanently exposed regardless of payment.

Unitel, Angola's largest mobile operator with roughly two-thirds market share, suffered a cyberattack that disrupted 2G, 3G, 4G and 5G service along with its mobile-payments platform, beginning just hours before the company's 28 July debut on Angola's stock exchange. Neither Unitel nor Angolan officials have published a detailed root-cause or attribution finding, and the company has not said whether subscriber data was affected.

Medium Incident

Threema absorbs a 30-hour, tactic-shifting DDoS campaign against consumer users

Secure-messaging provider Threema weathered a coordinated distributed-denial-of-service campaign over roughly 30 hours on 12–13 August that shifted attack techniques as defenses were applied, disrupting service for users in Switzerland, India and China. The company says its On-Prem product line — used by enterprise and government customers who host Threema's infrastructure inside their own environment — was unaffected because it sits outside the shared consumer infrastructure. No customer data was reported exposed; the campaign targeted availability, not data theft.

Critical Advisory

VMware vCenter RCE under active exploitation — 361 victim IPs across 47 countries and counting

A critical directory-traversal flaw in the vCenter Server syslog service gives unauthenticated remote code execution. Broadcom patched it 29 July; DFIR firm QUIRSO saw first contact with attacker infrastructure on 3 August and counted 361 distinct victim IPs across 47 countries by 7 August, over half in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France. The observed chain uses path traversal, a malicious cron job for persistence, and the open-source reverse_ssh framework for command and control; a suspected China-nexus operator is also deploying a Babuk-derived ransomware variant that researchers believe may be a smokescreen for deeper access. Many carriers run virtualized network functions — 4G/5G control- and user-plane elements, OSS/BSS and management systems — on vCenter-managed infrastructure, so compromise here reaches well beyond a single VM.

Citrix NetScaler pre-auth heap overflow gives unauthenticated root RCE — public PoC, no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation yet

A pre-authentication heap overflow in NetScaler ADC/Gateway's SAML signature canonicalization allows unauthenticated remote code execution at root. Security firm watchTowr published detailed technical analysis on 14 August; Citrix has fixed builds available (14.1-72.61 and 13.1-63.18). No confirmed in-the-wild exploitation has been reported yet, but the technical writeup materially raises that risk. NetScaler appliances are widely used by carriers and ISPs as remote-access gateways and load balancers for customer and OSS/BSS portals.
