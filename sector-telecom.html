Top Story

A China-nexus group is living inside Cisco IOS XR routers and TACACS servers, turning carrier-class network gear into a listening post

Sygnia has published incident-response research documenting new activity by Fire Ant, a China-nexus intrusion set whose operations the firm assesses as strongly overlapping with UNC3886 while stopping short of conclusive attribution. What makes it the lead item for this sector is that it is not another CVE. The group moved off the VMware hypervisor estates it was known for in 2025 and onto the network layer itself: Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS authentication servers and Linux management hosts. On the routers a custom implant ran behind a fake system service and executed only during alternating hours to limit its exposure to observation, while a second backdoor, BridgeAgent, presented itself as legitimate Zabbix monitoring software and supplied tunnelling and persistence. The operators created GRE tunnel interfaces that left no entry in configuration history, and suppressed the syslog messages that would have described them. A separate tool, TacTap, injected into the authentication process on TACACS servers to harvest credentials as they were used. The routers then became collection platforms: packet captures written on the device and uploaded to external FTP servers, exposing internal topology, administrative sessions, authentication flows, routing relationships and the traffic exchanged with connected networks. Sygnia describes a “target behind the target” pattern, in which trusted infrastructure is used to probe the high-value networks that connect to it over SSH, web services, SMB/RPC and RDP.

For a carrier or ISP this is the uncomfortable kind of intrusion report, because it lands on exactly the assets that usually sit outside the security programme rather than inside it. IOS XR is the operating system of the service-provider core; TACACS is the system that decides who may touch it. Compromise both and an adversary holds traffic visibility, credential flow and the authorisation layer at once. The second half of the finding is arguably worse than the first: the actor suppressed AAA requests, SNMP traps and command output, so the telemetry an operator would reach for to answer “has this happened to us” is the same telemetry the actor was editing. Sygnia’s guidance is to treat routers, TACACS servers, hypervisors and jump hosts as first-class forensic and Tier-0 assets rather than as plumbing — export their telemetry to independently administered systems, validate logs against more than one source of evidence, keep out-of-band access that does not traverse the equipment being investigated, and limit the blast radius of trust between connected networks. Sygnia declined to name the victim’s sector or location, and has published indicators of compromise, YARA rules and hunting guidance. There is no patch here, because no vulnerability was named: this is a hunting exercise, and the two Cisco IOS XR code-execution advisories further down this page are the reason not to defer it.

A second NetScaler flaw, this one an authentication bypass, went from public proof-of-concept to live exploitation in about a day

CVE-2026-19490 is a critical authentication bypass in Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway, disclosed in a Citrix advisory on 19 August 2026. It affects appliances configured as an AAA virtual server or as a Gateway — SSL VPN, ICA Proxy, CVPN or RDP Proxy — with the precise impact varying by firmware and SAML Action configuration. After a proof-of-concept appeared publicly, Ryan Dewhurst of Previdian recorded exploitation attempts from three countries on 3 September, and Belgium’s national cybersecurity centre issued its own exploitation warning the following day. This is the second NetScaler item on this page in the same window, and the pairing is the point: one flaw was under-rated and exploited six weeks after its patch, the other was correctly rated critical and exploited roughly a day after exploit code became public. Neither timeline leaves room for a monthly appliance cycle. Authentication bypass is also the worst failure mode for a device in this role, because it strips the control that everything behind the Gateway assumes is holding — MFA, conditional access, session identity — without generating a failed-login signal for anyone to notice.

Critical Incident

Attackers are exploiting an unauthenticated SQL injection in Sangoma Switchvox to reach code execution on enterprise phone systems

Last edition’s lead item, and still live. CVE-2026-9586 is an unauthenticated SQL injection in Sangoma Switchvox, the VoIP and unified-communications platform used to run enterprise PBX estates, rated CVSS 9.3 and exploited in the wild. The defect sits in an XML-processing endpoint that concatenates an unauthenticated PhoneIP parameter straight into PostgreSQL queries with no parameterisation, so an attacker who can reach the interface can run arbitrary SQL and pivot from there to code execution. Horizon3 confirmed live exploitation and published indicators of compromise; CISA added the CVE to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2 September with a 5 September federal remediation date, and reporting on that KEV batch describes post-exploitation activity including reverse shells and credential harvesting. It matters more on this page than on a general IT one because Switchvox is not general-purpose enterprise software — it is telephony infrastructure, deployed by carriers, MVNOs, hosted-voice providers and the channel partners who resell them, and it typically sits close to SIP trunks and provisioning data rather than in a hardened data-centre tier. A database compromise on a PBX is not just a server; it is extension mappings, SIP registration credentials, call detail records and voicemail — the raw materials for toll fraud, for caller-ID spoofing that survives downstream trust checks, and for quiet call-metadata collection. The federal deadline has now passed. Treat any internet-reachable Switchvox as compromised until proven otherwise, and start with the SIP and administrative credentials it holds rather than with the patch alone.

SonicWall SMA1000 remote-access appliances are being taken over by chaining two fresh zero-days into unauthenticated code execution

SonicWall has told customers that attackers are chaining two previously unknown flaws in SMA1000 secure-access appliances to achieve remote code execution. CVE-2026-83548 is a maximum-severity server-side request forgery in the Workplace interface that yields unauthorised access to sensitive functionality without credentials; CVE-2026-83549 is an OS command injection in the management console that normally requires administrative rights. Used together the first supplies the access the second assumes, and the pair becomes a pre-authentication path to the appliance. CISA added both to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 2 September with a 5 September remediation date. Affected models are the SMA1000 6210, 7210 and 8200v; SonicWall states the SSL-VPN and SMA 100 lines are not in scope. Shadowserver counts more than 400 internet-exposed SMA1000 appliances, and this is the latest in a run of SMA1000 zero-days exploited since July 2026, which is the part operators should weigh. A remote-access concentrator is where field engineers, NOC staff, roaming administrators and outsourced support terminate, so it holds the credentials and the network reach of everyone who uses it. A repeat-target appliance sitting at that junction warrants more than patching: assume the box has been reached, rotate what it authenticates, and check what those sessions could touch.

High Incident

Attackers added their own servers to Coder’s Cloudflare pool and served credential-stealing Terraform modules for fourteen hours

Coder has disclosed that an attacker gained access to its Cloudflare infrastructure and added unauthorised IP addresses to the pool serving its module registry. For roughly fourteen hours on 31 August 2026, between 07:35 and 21:45 UTC, Cloudflare routed a share of module requests to attacker-controlled servers that returned tampered Terraform modules. The malicious code was an information stealer aimed at exactly what a provisioning workflow has in memory: provisioner environment variables and secrets, cloud API keys, CI/CD credentials, SSH keys, OIDC tokens and database passwords. Coder has published patched releases 2.37.0, 2.36.4, 2.35.7 and 2.34.9, SQL queries to identify affected cached modules, and the attacker domain coder-infra[.]com as an indicator — but it has also been candid that it cannot see the attacker’s server logs and therefore cannot enumerate which deployments were served the malicious version. That candour is the operationally important part. For network operators the relevance is not the product so much as the pattern: infrastructure-as-code is how modern carriers build network automation, provisioning pipelines and lab environments, and the credentials a provisioner holds are frequently the ones with the broadest reach into cloud accounts and network tooling. A fourteen-hour window in a dependency-fetch path is long enough, and the absence of definitive telemetry means the safe assumption is exposure rather than a clean bill of health.

Citrix patched CVE-2026-8452 on 30 June 2026 and characterised it as a high-severity memory overflow leading to unpredictable behaviour and denial of service. Carried forward and re-checked this edition: the UNISOC modem research published by SSD Secure Disclosure on 17 August, credited to independent researcher 0x50594d, remains without a vendor fix. Medium Research

More than 5,400 hacked websites are serving ClickFix lures whose payload logic lives on a public blockchain, so there is no takedown target

Netskope has published analysis of a long-running campaign that has compromised over 5,400 websites, predominantly small-business WordPress and PrestaShop installations, and injected script that displays a ClickFix lure: a fake CAPTCHA instructing the visitor to open the Windows Run dialog and paste a PowerShell command. The distinguishing feature is where the payload logic lives. Rather than a hosted staging server, the operators store it in a smart contract on the BNB Smart Chain testnet — the technique commonly labelled EtherHiding — so the delivery infrastructure sits on a public, immutable, permissionless ledger. There is no host to seize, no registrar to notify and no domain to sinkhole, and the operators can change the payload by writing a new transaction. Netskope also documented an evolution in which the injected code opens a WebRTC peer connection and data channel, giving the attacker a covert encrypted path that blends into ordinary browser traffic rather than presenting as a conventional command-and-control beacon. Telemetry showed roughly 300 infected sites active on a given day, peaking at 536 endpoint calls during August. For carriers and ISPs the exposure runs in two directions: staff workstations meeting these lures during ordinary browsing, and hosting or web-services businesses whose customer sites are the compromised population being used to serve them.

Medium Research

A twelve-year-old PostgreSQL flaw lets a replication account load arbitrary code and become a permanent superuser

Researchers at Cyera have disclosed CVE-2026-6471, nicknamed PostGREShell, a missing-authorisation defect in PostgreSQL’s logical decoding that has been present since version 9.4. An account holding only replication privileges — a low-tier role usually granted to backup jobs, read replicas and change-data-capture pipelines — can pass an unsanitised filesystem path directly to dlopen(), bypassing the restriction that output plugins must load from an administrator-controlled directory. Code loaded that way runs inside the PostgreSQL address space with no sandbox and no checks on internal API calls, which Cyera turned into remote code execution, escalation to permanent superuser and a persistent backdoor. It is rated CVSS 7.2 because it requires that replication credential rather than being reachable unauthenticated. Fixes are in 18.6, 17.11, 16.15, 15.19 and 14.24; branches before 14 are affected but out of support. No exploitation has been reported. The reason it earns space on a telecom page is the trust assumption it breaks. Replication accounts are handed out freely, held in automation and backup tooling rather than in a vault, and rarely rotated, because the role has always been understood as read-only movement of data. In carrier environments those databases hold subscriber records, CDRs, provisioning state and billing — and the account assumed to be able only to copy them turns out to be able to own the server.

The advisory describes memory corruption and memory-safety defects together with improper access control, and the outcome is remote code execution on affected devices. It leads the advisories section on the telecom page because of where IOS XR runs. This is not enterprise switching software; it is the operating system on service-provider aggregation, core and peering routers — the platforms carrying customer traffic, terminating peering and transit sessions, and holding the BGP state that determines where prefixes go. Code execution on a router in that position is not a compromised server. It is the ability to alter forwarding, mirror traffic, manipulate routing advertisements, or simply drop a device that a large customer population depends on, and it does so from a platform that is frequently reachable from peers and transit partners rather than only from an internal management network. The other complication is change control: core routers are exactly the estate where patching is slowest, because the maintenance window is negotiated and the blast radius of a bad upgrade is enormous. Start the change process now, and in the meantime verify that management-plane access is filtered to your own address space.

CVE-2026-20212 is a CVSS 9.8 flaw in Silicon One-based Cisco Nexus 9000 switches caused by a service binding to an unrestricted IP address. Operators build careful separation between management and data planes, and a service silently listening in the default routing instance defeats that design by putting an unauthenticated root path on whatever that instance can reach. In carrier data centres these switches carry customer-facing and internal fabric traffic alike, so the practical question is not whether you patch but what is currently able to send a packet to those two ports. France’s national cybersecurity agency ANSSI, chairing the G7 Cybersecurity Working Group, has published a call to action urging governments and organisations to accelerate the move to quantum-safe encryption. Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the UK and the US signed, with endorsement from the European Commission and ENISA. The document names critical infrastructure generally rather than telecom specifically, but carriers sit unusually exposed: long-lived transport encryption, subscriber authentication material and interconnect trust all rest on cryptography with equipment refresh cycles measured in many years.
