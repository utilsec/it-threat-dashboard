An unauthenticated command-injection flaw in Zimbra is being exploited — and the affected install base is heavy in government, education and legal

CERT Polska has confirmed in-the-wild exploitation of CVE-2026-73570, a command-injection flaw in Zimbra Collaboration Suite carrying a CVSS of 8.9. A crafted SMTP request reaches the SNMP notification path and executes operating-system commands as the zimbra user, with no authentication required. The affected configuration is specific rather than universal — ZCS below 10.1.20 with the zimbra-snmp package installed and SNMP notifications enabled — but that combination is common on long-lived on-premises deployments, which is exactly where Zimbra concentrates. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 21 August with a federal remediation deadline of 24 August, an unusually short window that reflects how little work the exploit requires. The reason this matters more than its CVSS suggests is the install base: Zimbra is disproportionately run by public-sector bodies, universities, law firms and nonprofits that chose it to avoid Microsoft licensing, and those organisations tend to have the smallest patch teams and the longest change windows. A mail server executing attacker commands is also a near-ideal foothold — it holds credentials, it is trusted by every internal system, and its outbound traffic looks legitimate.

A compromised maintainer account poisoned a Rust crate with 245 million downloads — the malicious build was live for under two hours

Attackers took over a maintainer account on crates.io and published malicious versions of three Rust packages: arrayref 0.3.10, internment 0.8.7 and append-only-vec 0.1.9. The significant one is arrayref, with roughly 245 million all-time and 53.9 million ninety-day downloads — a foundational dependency that appears deep in Rust build graphs rather than in anyone’s direct requirements. The mechanism is worth understanding because it targeted the build rather than the runtime: the poisoned crates pulled in a typosquatted proc-macro1 package whose build script fetched a second-stage implant that harvested credentials from Chrome, Brave and Edge profiles on the build machine. Developer workstations and CI runners hold cloud tokens, signing keys and source access, which makes them a higher-value target than most production hosts. The malicious versions were live for between 86 and 107 minutes before removal, and no evidence has emerged that a poisoned build reached production anywhere. Wiz reported infrastructure overlap with recent North Korean supply-chain activity; no vendor has made a formal attribution, and it should be treated as unconfirmed. There is no CVE, because nothing was vulnerable — the package was simply replaced.

CISA has added two TrueConf Server flaws to the KEV catalog with a remediation deadline of 3 September. CVE-2026-72529 is a missing-authentication weakness: an unauthenticated attacker who can reach TCP 4307 invokes an undocumented function to execute an arbitrary script. CVE-2026-72530 is a code-injection flaw that breaks that script out of its isolated environment and onto the host. Chained, they give unauthenticated remote code execution on the server. Self-hosted conferencing servers are typically DMZ-facing, are frequently used to run vendor and support sessions, and are rarely on anyone’s critical-asset list.

High Research

Researchers found 9,300 live AWS access keys in public artifacts — 88% still worked, and 768 gave full account control

Truffle Security published an analysis of AWS access keys exposed in public sources between August 2022 and August 2026, and the finding is not that keys leak — everyone knows keys leak — but that they stay valid. Of more than 9,300 exposed keys, 88% were still active when tested on 10 August 2026. Among them: 526 root account keys, 242 keys belonging to IAM users with administrative policies, and 768 keys granting full control of the account they belonged to. Some 817 were traceable to identifiable companies. The single largest source was Hugging Face, accounting for 8,482 unique key exposures, which points at the specific failure mode — keys committed into model repositories, notebooks and published build artifacts rather than into obvious source control. That is a category most secret-scanning programmes do not cover, because it sits outside the code repository entirely. The operational lesson is about lifecycle rather than hygiene: an exposed key that nobody rotates is not a past incident, it is a standing credential, and a four-year-old leak is as exploitable as this morning’s.

Microsoft Copilot Personal flaws allowed one-click data exfiltration from connected apps

Researchers disclosed flaws in Microsoft Copilot Personal that could let an attacker exfiltrate data from the applications a user has connected to the assistant with a single click. It arrives as part of a mid-August cluster of AI-assistant findings — alongside issues reported under the names CoSnitch and TwinLoot, and separate research on secret exposure in Model Context Protocol integrations — and the pattern across them is consistent: the assistant is granted broad, persistent access to mail, files and chat on the user’s behalf, so a one-click or injection flaw in the assistant inherits that access wholesale. AI integrations should therefore be inventoried and governed as a data-egress channel with its own permissions and logging, rather than treated as a productivity feature enabled per user.

Restrict third-party connector installation to an approved list, log assistant data access the way you would any other egress path, and review whether personal-tier AI accounts are being used against corporate data at all.

CISA adds four actively-exploited flaws to KEV with a 21 August deadline — Windows IKEEXT, vCenter, SharePoint and macOS Screen Sharing

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 August, all with a 21 August remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies. They are CVE-2026-33824, a double-free flaw in the Microsoft Windows IKE service extensions (IKEEXT) leading to remote code execution; CVE-2026-59310, the VMware vCenter path-traversal flaw already being exploited at scale (see the top story); CVE-2026-55040, a weak-authentication issue in Microsoft SharePoint that permits a security-feature bypass; and CVE-2026-65400, an improper-authentication flaw in Apple macOS Screen Sharing. Separately, CISA updated its KEV entry for CVE-2025-60710, a link-following privilege-escalation flaw in the Windows Task Host that takes a low-privileged local user to SYSTEM on Windows 11 and Server 2025, to note that it is now being abused by ransomware gangs; Microsoft patched that one in November 2025 and it entered KEV in April 2026, so it is a hunt-for-stragglers signal rather than a new patch cycle. The common thread is that all four additions sit in software most enterprises run somewhere — the Windows VPN stack, the virtualization control plane, the intranet platform and the Mac fleet.

Critical Advisory

GitLab ships an emergency patch for a critical code-injection flaw plus a zero-click GraphQL bug that could delete public projects

GitLab released an out-of-band security update on 18 August covering a critical code-injection vulnerability and, separately, a zero-click flaw in its GraphQL API that could allow an unauthenticated attacker to delete public projects. The zero-click characteristic is what makes this awkward operationally: there is no user interaction to train against and no obvious phishing precursor to detect, so interim mitigation is limited and patching is effectively the only control. Self-managed GitLab instances are the exposure here — GitLab.com is operated by the vendor, but self-hosted servers are usually the crown-jewel system for an engineering organisation, holding source, CI/CD runners, deployment credentials and container registries. Anyone running a self-managed instance should treat this as an emergency change rather than a scheduled upgrade.

No confirmed in-the-wild exploitation reported as of this edition — emergency vendor release Not listed in CISA KEV as of this edition
Emergency patch released by GitLab, 18 Aug 2026 Action: Upgrade self-managed GitLab instances to the patched releases now. Because the GraphQL flaw needs no authentication or user action, do not rely on awareness or WAF rules as a stopgap; if you cannot patch immediately, restrict the GraphQL endpoint and the instance itself to trusted networks. Afterwards, verify that recent project deletions and CI/CD credential use look legitimate.

Critical Advisory

A max-severity SAP Commerce Cloud flaw went from patch to exploitation in three days, with a public exploit the day after

CVE-2026-58231 is a CVSS 10.0 flaw in SAP Commerce Cloud combining insufficient authorisation with inadequate input validation, allowing arbitrary code execution against internal components. The timeline is the story: SAP patched it on 11 August, exploitation attempts were observed on 14 August, and a public proof-of-concept was circulating by 15 August. Shadowserver counted more than 4,200 internet-exposed SAP Commerce Cloud instances at the time of reporting. For a platform that sits directly in front of customer and transaction data and connects back into core SAP environments, a four-day gap between fix and working public exploit leaves very little room for a normal change-approval cycle.

Critical Advisory

Metabase’s CVSS-10 SQL injection was exploited as a zero-day — Framework and Trezor’s fulfilment partner both confirmed breaches

CVE-2026-72898, an unauthenticated SQL injection in Metabase’s password-reset endpoint that yields administrative access, carries the maximum CVSS score of 10.0 and was added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August. It affects open-source Metabase 0.58.0 through 0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0 through 1.63.x before 1.63.5, and attackers exploited it before a patch existed. Two victims have confirmed breaches: laptop maker Framework, whose notice covers names, emails, addresses, phone numbers and billing information for its customer base, and ShipMonk, the fulfilment partner for hardware-wallet maker Trezor, affecting roughly 13,689 Trezor customers — Trezor has said no seed phrases or private keys were exposed. Business-intelligence tools like Metabase are worth re-examining generally: they usually hold standing read credentials to the databases that matter most.

Critical Advisory

The macOS Screen Sharing authentication bypass is now in CISA KEV — roughly 40,000 Macs were still reachable on port 5900

CVE-2026-65400 is an improper-authentication flaw in macOS Screen Sharing, rated CVSS 9.8, that CISA added to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 August with a 21 August federal deadline. Attackers have been scanning the internet for Macs with the Screen Sharing port (5900) exposed and using the bypass to obtain root access and install Monero cryptomining malware; the Netherlands’ NCSC-NL estimated roughly 40,000 Macs still reachable via the service after Apple’s fix. Apple patched the flaw on 6 August in macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9 and Sonoma 14.8.9. Cryptomining is the payload being observed, but the access obtained is root on an endpoint — a foothold that is equally usable for credential theft or lateral movement. Enterprises running Mac fleets, especially shared lab machines or remote-access jump hosts, should not read this as a consumer-only problem.

CVE-2026-65400 Apple macOS • CISA KEV
Active — internet-wide scanning for exposed port 5900, root access obtained and Monero miners deployed Added to CISA KEV 18 Aug 2026; federal deadline 21 Aug 2026
Fixed 6 Aug 2026 in macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9, Sonoma 14.8.9 Action: Push the patched macOS builds to every managed Mac, and disable Screen Sharing or restrict it behind VPN rather than exposing 5900 to the internet. Scan your external attack surface for open 5900 now, and on any Mac that was exposed, check for unexpected launch agents, unfamiliar processes and sustained CPU load before assuming a patch is sufficient.

High Advisory

A Ray code-injection flaw enters KEV — a malicious web page plus DNS rebinding can reach a developer’s local Ray cluster

CVE-2025-62593, a code-injection flaw in the Ray distributed-compute framework rated CVSS 9.4, was added to CISA’s KEV catalog on 17 August with a 20 August federal deadline. The underlying design issue is that Ray ships with no authentication on critical endpoints, so a developer simply browsing a malicious web page or loading a malicious advertisement can have that page use DNS rebinding to reach the Ray instance running on their own machine or local network and execute code on it. That makes this exploitable without any inbound network exposure, which defeats the usual “it’s only bound to localhost” reasoning. It is fixed in Ray 2.52.0. Ray clusters typically sit alongside training data and hold cloud credentials for object storage, so the blast radius is not limited to the developer’s workstation.

CVE-2025-62593 Ray • CISA KEV
Active — added to KEV on evidence of exploitation; DNS-rebinding path works against local, non-internet-facing instances Added to CISA KEV 17 Aug 2026; federal deadline 20 Aug 2026

Put authentication in front of Ray dashboards and endpoints, and rotate any cloud credentials that Ray workers hold.

High Advisory

An Adobe Commerce flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker switch a shopping session into another customer’s account

CVE-2026-71362 in Adobe Commerce and Magento allows an unauthenticated attacker to switch a customer session over to a different customer’s account — no account of their own, no administrative rights and no user interaction required. Sansec has observed exploitation attempts. The practical consequence for a storefront is direct access to order history, saved addresses and stored payment methods belonging to other shoppers, and it is not the kind of abuse that leaves obvious traces in application logs, because the requests look like ordinary session activity. Any Adobe Commerce or Magento operator should treat this as a patch-this-week item and then work out whether customer accounts were accessed, since breach-notification duties may attach.

CVE-2026-71362 Adobe Commerce / Magento
Active — exploitation attempts observed by Sansec; no authentication or user interaction required Not listed in CISA KEV as of this edition
Vendor fix available — apply the Adobe Commerce/Magento security update Action: Apply the Adobe update to all storefronts, then invalidate existing customer sessions so any hijacked session is dropped. Review access logs for session-identifier anomalies and unusual account-switching patterns, and involve privacy or legal counsel early if there is evidence customer accounts were reached.

High Advisory

August Patch Tuesday’s one exploited zero-day is a Windows WinSock privilege-escalation flaw tied to a Lazarus campaign

Microsoft’s August 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 398 CVEs, of which exactly one was under active exploitation at release: CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free vulnerability in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (afd.sys), rated CVSS 7.0. It is a local elevation-of-privilege flaw taking an attacker who already has code execution up to SYSTEM, not an unauthenticated remote entry point. Check Point ties exploitation to North Korea’s Lazarus Group and its “Operation Dream Job” campaign against defence and aerospace organisations in France, Germany, Brazil and India, delivered via trojanised PDF viewers and DLL side-loading. CISA added the flaw to KEV on 11 August with a 25 August deadline. Because the danger is realised only after initial access, patching should be paired with the phishing and side-loading defences that block the delivery chain.

The new National Security Science and Technology Strategy names cybersecurity, OT/ICS resilience and technology supply chains as explicit priorities

The National Security Science and Technology Strategy, released on 19 August, sets out four pillars and names cybersecurity, operational-technology and industrial-control-system resilience, and technology supply chains among its explicit priorities. It is a strategy document rather than a rule — nothing in it changes an obligation on any organisation today — but strategies of this kind tend to precede funding lines, procurement language and eventually sector requirements. The practical read for IT leaders is directional: supply-chain assurance for technology components, and the security of the boundary between enterprise IT and operational technology, are both on a trajectory toward more formal expectation rather than less. Organisations in critical-infrastructure supply chains may find these themes appearing in customer questionnaires and contract terms well before any regulation does.

Strategy document — not tied to any vulnerability or active exploitation Not applicable
Policy direction, not a patchable issue Action: Skim the strategy for the pillars that touch your sector and use them to sanity-check your own roadmap, particularly technology supply-chain assurance and IT/OT boundary security. If you supply into critical infrastructure, expect these themes in customer security questionnaires before they appear in any binding requirement.

A UK regulator warns professionals over putting confidential client data into public AI tools — a governance problem every IT team shares

The UK Solicitors Regulation Authority issued a warning notice on 17 August, reported the following day, after finding solicitors submitting fabricated case law to courts and entering confidential client information into public AI tools. The SRA’s position is that practitioners remain fully accountable for AI output and must be able to demonstrate human oversight and risk-based governance. The specific regulator is sector-bound, but the underlying issue is not: any organisation with confidentiality obligations — to clients, patients, employees or contract counterparties — faces the same exposure when staff paste sensitive material into consumer AI services that were never covered by a data-processing agreement. It pairs directly with this edition’s Copilot findings: unsanctioned AI use and over-permissioned sanctioned AI are two halves of the same data-egress problem.

Regulatory guidance — not tied to a vulnerability or active exploitation Not applicable
Mitigate via AI-use policy and technical controls, not a patch Action: Publish a plain-language AI-use policy that states what categories of data may never leave sanctioned tools, provide an approved enterprise AI option so staff are not driven to consumer services, and add data-loss-prevention coverage for public AI endpoints. Keep an auditable record of human review where AI output feeds regulated or client-facing work.
Suggested SLA: AI-use policy and DLP coverage review — 30 days Owner:
