Attackers are breaching MSPs through ScreenConnect: Huntress counts six MSPs, 286 endpoints and 47 downstream organisations and rising, and ConnectWise has disabled five third-party extensions across about 400 partner environments

Huntress saw a spike in attacks on ScreenConnect partner instances on 13 November. BleepingComputer, citing Huntress, puts the toll at six MSPs, 286 endpoints and 47 organisations and rising; The Hacker News reports 205 hosts at 41 organisations. The attackers abused ScreenConnect’s Run Command feature to push rogue ScreenConnect clients onto customer machines — misuse of a legitimate feature rather than a ScreenConnect bug — and the suspected way in is an authentication flaw in the third-party RMM+ extension (versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32), which has no CVE. ConnectWise responded by disabling five extensions (Labtech Plus, RMM Plus, RMM+ and two Password Link variants) across roughly 400 partner environments. For persistence the intruders installed a fake “Windows Update Agent” service, UpdateConnect.ClientService.exe. A worm-like toolkit called SCKit can also abuse ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9, fixed in 26.6.5), but there is no evidence it was used here. The activity is unattributed, and The Hacker News does not link it to Storm-0940, the actor behind October’s ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise. This leads over the VMware vCenter zero-day and N-able’s 4EverAble2Shell attacks (both below) because it is a confirmed, still-growing compromise of MSPs that hands attackers their customers’ endpoints in every sector.
