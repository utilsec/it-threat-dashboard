Ivanti Connect Secure chain gives attackers root on fully patched VPNs; one flaw is unpatched and Ivanti’s checker may miss compromise

Ivanti disclosed on 13 October 2026 that attackers had chained three flaws in Connect Secure to run commands as root on appliances that were fully up to date. Two of the flaws are fixed under a single CVE, CVE-2026-104935 (CVSS 3.1 8.6); the third, a privilege escalation, has no CVE and no patch yet. Volexity found the chain while responding to an intrusion and tracks the actor as UTA0576, a newly identified cluster; exploitation dates to at least early October. CISA listed the CVE in KEV on 13 October with a 16 October deadline and warns that Ivanti’s Integrity Checker may not detect this activity. Many hospitals rely on Connect Secure for clinician and vendor remote access — an editorial observation.

South African pathology lab Ampath confirms a cyber incident and has told the Information Regulator; an extortion actor claims 80 million records, a figure Ampath says materially overstates who is affected

Ampath Laboratories, one of South Africa’s largest pathology providers, has confirmed a cybersecurity incident that may have exposed personal information, after an actor calling itself ByteToBreach posted screenshots on a hacking forum on 13 October 2026. Ampath says the incident is contained, external forensic specialists are involved, and it has notified the Information Regulator under section 22 of POPIA. The data that may be affected includes names, ID numbers, dates of birth, contact details and medical-aid and payment information, though not every record holds every field. In a follow-up statement on 14 October Ampath said it had found no evidence that medical records, diagnoses, pathology results or banking details were compromised. ByteToBreach claims to have copied 10 of 375 databases, covering employee, patient, finance and directory data, says an admission table alone holds more than 80 million records, and says negotiations with Ampath were short. Ampath counters that one patient can generate many records over time, so the figure materially overstates the number of people affected. The attacker’s claims are unverified, and MyBroadband reports that a malware scan of one posted “sample” download flagged backdoors and trojans, so the samples are a hazard in themselves. Ampath’s website went offline briefly on 13 October; no disruption to testing or results has been reported. For any lab or hospital the useful lesson is the one Ampath drew in public: a leak-site row count is not a count of patients.

Italy’s data protection authority, the Garante, announced on 2 October 2026 a €7 million fine against IQVIA Solutions Italy, part of the health-data analytics and clinical-research group, in a decision dated 23 September. CISA urges critical infrastructure and small security teams to deploy cyber decoys — honeytokens and tripwires as a cheap, high-confidence detection layer

CISA published guidance on 17 September 2026 encouraging critical infrastructure operators, and small and medium-sized organisations new to deception, to deploy cyber decoys: honeytokens and tripwires such as fake files, fake accounts and planted credentials placed in high-value parts of the network. Because nothing legitimate should ever touch them, any use raises a high-confidence alert. The guidance recommends mapping decoy coverage against MITRE ATT&CK and MITRE Engage and refining placement through threat emulation, and it positions deception as a supplement to Zero Trust rather than a replacement. For hospitals and small practices the appeal is the ratio of cost to signal. Several intrusions on this page were found late — zHealth’s data copying was discovered roughly five months after access — and few small health IT teams can staff round-the-clock log analysis. A handful of well-placed decoys needs almost no tuning: a fake privileged service account in Active Directory, a honeytoken credential in the file share that holds report exports, a decoy patient-list spreadsheet on the shared drive and a canary API key in the integration engine’s configuration. Each turns a quiet discovery phase into a single alert that is rarely a false positive. The part to get right is the routing: send those alerts somewhere that is watched out of hours, and make sure your managed service provider knows what they mean and what to do first.
