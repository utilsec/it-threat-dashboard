High Incident — Process abuse

Revolut was tricked into handing over identity documents and full transaction history by an attacker impersonating a government agency — no software flaw was involved

Revolut disclosed on 14 September that an unauthorised party impersonated a government agency and, using what the company describes as valid technical domain credentials, submitted fraudulent data requests that its process treated as legitimate. High Incident — Credential theft campaign

A phishing-as-a-service platform called BigBear 2.0 bypassed MFA at 258 organisations and collected more than 5,000 Microsoft 365 logins

Researchers detailed a phishing-as-a-service operation, BigBear 2.0, that defeated multi-factor authentication at 258 organisations and harvested over 5,000 Microsoft 365 credentials. The technique is adversary-in-the-middle: the victim reaches a proxy that relays the real Microsoft sign-in page, so the login looks correct, the MFA prompt is genuine and the user approves it — and the operator captures the resulting session token rather than the password. A stolen token is a working session, which is why resetting a password after this kind of compromise often changes nothing. Sold as a service, the platform puts this capability in reach of operators with no development ability of their own, which is how the victim count reaches the hundreds. The defensive answer is not more user training but authentication that cannot be relayed and session controls that notice when a token turns up somewhere implausible.

The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s reporting duty is now live — 24 hours to notify an actively exploited vulnerability in anything you sell with software in it

The incident-reporting provisions of the EU Cyber Resilience Act took effect this week. Manufacturers of products with digital elements placed on the EU market must now notify serious security incidents and actively exploited vulnerabilities within a 24-hour window. The obligation lands on a wider population than most organisations assume, because it follows the product rather than the sector: if your company sells anything containing software or connectivity into the EU — equipment, appliances, connected devices, embedded components, packaged software — you are a manufacturer for these purposes, whatever the rest of the business does. Twenty-four hours is short enough that it cannot be met by a process invented after the fact. It has to be decided in advance who declares, on what evidence, and to which authority, and the answer has to work at 6pm on a Friday. The practical difficulty is rarely the notification form; it is the ambiguity in the middle of an incident, when you know something is being exploited but not yet how widely, and the clock has already started.
