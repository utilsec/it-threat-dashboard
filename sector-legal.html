Top Story

UK regulator reprimands ACRO, the Criminal Records Office, over three intrusions that went undetected for two years

The UK Information Commissioner's Office issued a formal reprimand to ACRO, the Criminal Records Office, after three separate intrusions between July 2021 and June 2023 went undetected for roughly two years. Attackers exploited an unpatched Kentico content-management system on ACRO's public-facing applicant portal, which had run the same version since September 2019 despite known vulnerabilities, with responsibility for patching left unclear inside the organisation. Trend Micro antivirus tooling on affected systems generated repeated warnings during the attack window — including four separate detections of the Mimikatz credential-harvesting tool — and none of them were acted on. The most serious of the three intrusions maintained persistent access to ACRO's network for approximately seven months, from August 2022 to March 2023. The ICO found that data on nearly 11,000 people was staged for exfiltration, including personal information belonging to survivors of domestic violence who had used ACRO's services for background-check and safeguarding purposes; ACRO separately notified more than 84,000 people who had submitted applications during the vulnerable window. The regulator issued a reprimand rather than a fine. For a legal-sector audience the case is less about a novel attack technique than about what a sustained detection failure looks like from the inside: a known-vulnerable CMS, an ownership gap for patching, and alert fatigue that let confirmed credential-theft tooling run unchallenged for months. The harm profile — background-check and safeguarding data tied to vulnerable individuals — is exactly the kind of sensitive record legal-adjacent and justice-system IT environments routinely hold.

Ransomware disrupts Colombia's Ministry of Justice five days before presidential transition

A ransomware attack struck Colombia's Ministry of Justice on 2 August 2026, encrypting files and disrupting systems used for drug monitoring and legal-case processing. The timing put the incident five days ahead of the country's presidential transition, adding operational pressure to the ministry's response; that timing is noted here only as operational context. Acting Justice Minister Cielo Rusinque said some files were encrypted but that officials had verified no data was captured or exfiltrated — an assessment that has not been independently confirmed. The attack follows a string of intrusions against Colombian government and critical-infrastructure targets this year, including a March breach at the national tax authority DIAN and a July compromise affecting more than a dozen Ecopetrol subsidiaries; researchers point to Colombian organisations expanding cloud infrastructure faster than their security capabilities as a contributing factor. For legal-sector readers, the case is a reminder that court and justice-ministry IT — case-management systems, drug-monitoring platforms and the legal-process workflows that depend on them — sits squarely inside ransomware crews' target set, with downtime landing directly on active legal proceedings.

Medium Third-Party Breach

Contractor breach exposes ~300 Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service employee records in Scotland

Scotland's Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) disclosed on 13 August 2026 that a third-party contractor running a mandatory Data Maturity Assessment for Scottish public bodies suffered a data breach, after suspicious activity was detected on 5 August. Roughly 300 COPFS employees had names, job titles and work email addresses exposed; COPFS says case, victim and witness data was not affected. Because multiple Scottish government agencies use the same contractor for the same assessment programme, the exposure may extend beyond COPFS to other agencies that submitted data through it — the full scope had not been confirmed as of the report. Security researchers flagged the familiar shape of the problem: point-in-time vendor vetting leaves suppliers unmonitored between assessments, which is exactly the gap this incident exposed. Even limited employee data — names, roles and email addresses — is useful reconnaissance for targeted phishing against a prosecution service, where a single compromised account can be a route toward more sensitive systems.

Hackers breach Liechtenstein's foundations and trusts registry, accessing data on ~31,000 entities

Attackers penetrated Liechtenstein's confidential register of foundations and trusts in late July 2026, gaining access to information on roughly 31,000 registered entities. Exposed data reportedly includes beneficial owners' names, dates of birth, nationality and place of residence; the government says no financial data, addresses or phone numbers were accessed. Prime Minister Brigitte Haas said the country will not pay a ransom if one is demanded and that officials are “examining various scenarios” for how the incident develops. The registry was established in 2021 as part of Liechtenstein's move to comply with international financial-transparency and anti-money-laundering standards after the jurisdiction's history as a low-transparency financial haven. Officials plan to rebuild the registry online, saying continued access for international financial authorities outweighs the risk of a repeat incident. For legal practices doing trust, estate and beneficial-ownership work, or AML/KYC due diligence touching Liechtenstein-registered structures, this is a direct hit on a source register those workflows rely on.

High Research

Akira affiliate disables EDR by forcing Windows into Safe Mode after breaching a SonicWall VPN without MFA

Huntress documented an Akira ransomware affiliate that gained initial access through an internet-facing SonicWall VPN device with no multi-factor authentication configured, then moved from VPN access to a domain controller via RDP within two hours. The attacker enumerated Active Directory, moved laterally to an application server, and used WinRAR and the s5cmd tool to exfiltrate files to an attacker-controlled S3 bucket before installing AnyDesk for persistent remote access. The notable step: the attacker forced the compromised host into Windows Safe Mode with Networking — a roughly ten-minute window in which EDR did not run and antivirus was blind — and added AnyDesk to the Safe Mode registry so remote access would survive a reboot. When the attacker tried to run the Akira encryptor payload inside Safe Mode, it failed for insufficient virtual memory, so the intrusion ended in data theft without encryption, but the attacker still had credentials and files to extort with, all inside about five hours. Huntress recommends MFA on every VPN account and monitoring for unexpected Safe Mode configuration changes. Professional-services networks, including law firms, are a standard target for remote-access-first ransomware playbooks like this one, and unauthenticated or single-factor VPN access remains the most common way in.

Confirmed technique — Akira affiliate; encryption failed, data still stolen TTP, not a specific CVE
No patch — configuration & EDR-tamper detection Action: Require MFA on every remote-access VPN account without exception, and add detection for hosts booting into or reconfigured for Safe Mode with Networking outside a change window.

Medium Research

“City-Forum” actor spends over a year scraping data through misconfigured Salesforce and ServiceNow guest portals

Researchers describe a financially motivated actor, tracked via infrastructure linked to a domain associated with it since March 2025, that has spent more than a year abusing overly permissive guest-user access on Salesforce Experience Cloud and ServiceNow Service Portal sites to enumerate and bulk-extract customer records. On Salesforce the technique targets the Aura framework and newer Lightning Web Runtime sites through their public-facing configuration and GraphQL endpoints; on ServiceNow it abuses a search endpoint that accepts anonymous requests when guest access is enabled. This is a configuration-abuse campaign, not a vulnerability being exploited — the sites are working as configured, just configured too permissively. Confirmed targets reported so far span telecommunications, banking and financial services, enterprise software vendors, and public-sector portals; activity was described as still running and climbing in volume as of the report, with no legal-sector victim named. Many law firms run Salesforce- or ServiceNow-based client-intake and matter portals with guest or unauthenticated access enabled by design, which makes this exactly the kind of configuration review worth running even absent a confirmed legal-sector victim.

Confirmed — active, ongoing campaign; no legal-sector victim confirmed yet Configuration abuse, not a CVE
No patch — guest-access configuration review Action: Audit guest-user sharing rules on any Salesforce Experience Cloud or ServiceNow Service Portal site used for client intake, and restrict object and field visibility to only what an unauthenticated visitor needs.

Metabase hits CISA KEV after zero-day exploitation — a direct hit on matter and billing analytics stacks

CVE-2026-72898 (CVSS 10.0) is an unauthenticated SQL injection in Metabase's /reset_password endpoint that grants full administrative access. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 August 2026 with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August, after confirming it was exploited as a zero-day before a patch existed. It affects open-source Metabase 0.58.0 through 0.63.x before 0.63.5 and Enterprise 1.58.0 through 1.63.x before 1.63.5. Confirmed victims include laptop maker Framework, whose full customer base had names, emails, addresses, phone numbers and billing information exposed, and ShipMonk, Trezor's fulfilment partner, affecting roughly 13,689 Trezor customers (no seed phrases or keys). Metabase is a common choice for firms building self-service dashboards over matter, billing and time-and-expense data, often connected directly to case-management or financial databases — an admin-takeover flaw in that layer exposes exactly the analytics most legal operations teams have built without formal IT sign-off.

Critical Advisory

Suspected China-nexus actor exploits VMware vCenter flaw at scale — 361 victim IPs across 47 countries

CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8) is a critical directory-traversal flaw in the vCenter Server syslog service that allows unauthenticated remote code execution as root. Broadcom patched it 29 July 2026; DFIR firm QUIRSO observed first contact with attacker infrastructure on 3 August, and by 7 August had counted 361 distinct victim IP addresses across 47 countries, concentrated in Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France. The attack chain uses the path-traversal flaw, then a malicious cron job for persistence, then the open-source reverse_ssh framework for command and control. QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence — based on Chinese-language artifacts in attacker scripts and activity aligned with UTC+8 working hours — that a China-nexus operator is behind the campaign, which has deployed a Babuk-derived ransomware variant on ESXi hosts that researchers believe may be a smokescreen for deeper intrusion rather than the actual goal. The flaw is not yet in CISA KEV. vCenter runs the virtualization management plane for most enterprise environments — guest VMs, datastores and, in many deployments, backup infrastructure — so a law firm or legal-services provider running its own virtualized case-management, document-management or e-discovery environment on vCenter is directly in scope.

High Advisory

Cisco ASA/FTD remote-access flaw crashes firewalls in the wild — added to CISA KEV

CVE-2026-20349 (CVSS 8.6) is an insufficient-error-checking flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and Firepower Threat Defense that lets an unauthenticated attacker crash the device with a crafted HTTP request where remote-access services are enabled and SSL listen sockets are exposed. Cisco published the advisory on 11 August 2026 and confirmed exploitation attempts in the wild; CISA added it to KEV the same day with a federal remediation deadline of 14 August. Affected trains span ASA 9.16 through 9.24 and FTD 7.0 through 10.0. Hotfixes are available and Cisco states there is no workaround. ASA/FTD devices are a common choice for law-firm remote-access VPNs and perimeter firewalls; a denial-of-service flaw here is an availability problem on the exact path attorneys, paralegals and litigation-support staff use to reach case files remotely.

High Advisory

Pre-auth NetScaler heap overflow gives unauthenticated root RCE — public PoC, no confirmed exploitation yet

CVE-2026-8452 is a pre-authentication heap overflow in Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway's SAML signature canonicalization that researchers at watchTowr showed can be turned into unauthenticated root remote code execution — crafting an oversized attribute inside the SAML SignedInfo element to corrupt memory, overwrite a function pointer and redirect execution to attacker shellcode. watchTowr published a detailed technical writeup on 14 August 2026. Citrix has fixed the issue in NetScaler 14.1-72.61 and 13.1-63.18. As of this writing there is a public proof of concept but no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation, which distinguishes it from the actively-exploited flaws elsewhere on this page — but NetScaler's history with prior SAML and gateway flaws being weaponized quickly means that gap is not expected to last. Law firms running NetScaler as a remote-access gateway or SSO front door should treat this as a near-term patch, not a routine one.

Policy Litigation

Change Healthcare MDL judge imposes strict new rules on handling the stolen dataset

The federal judge overseeing multidistrict litigation from the 2024 Change Healthcare ransomware breach approved a court order, reported 11 August 2026, setting out seven security rules governing how the stolen dataset can be used during the case. UnitedHealth may hand over only one copy of the full dataset, on an encrypted drive with the password transmitted separately; recipients must re-encrypt it and may only access it from computers fully disconnected from any network, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth disabled. No complete copies may be made — only samples of 25 or fewer individuals’ records, under matching protections — every transfer must be logged, security incidents reported within two days, and all copies destroyed within 30 days of the case's conclusion. The data cannot be used to recruit additional plaintiffs. For legal-sector readers this is a concrete, court-imposed template for how breach-derived datasets should be handled inside litigation and e-discovery workflows — a live example of courts, not just regulators, imposing security obligations directly on how firms manage sensitive litigation data.
