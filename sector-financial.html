Correction: earlier editions of this page carried Bitget’s revised figure of about $390.06 million; this edition uses Mandiant’s range. High Breach

Revolut’s forged government requests came from a hijacked Italian Interior Ministry certified-mail account — about 680 high-value customers exposed over five to six months, and the breach now has a second act: a smishing wave that lands inside genuine Revolut text threads and fakes the video identity check — and on 25 September a second notice: DriveWealth, the US broker that once carried Revolut customers’ US-stock trades, was breached through social engineering

Revolut has confirmed that fraudsters sent it fake government data requests, that its compliance process accepted them as genuine, and that customer data was released. The disclosure was first reported on 12 September 2026 and widely covered on 14 September. Revolut’s own description is that “an unauthorized third party utilized a legitimate government agency domain email to submit fraudulent requests for information”. Updated 17–18 September: SecurityWeek, citing Hudson Rock and other investigators, reports that the domain was genuine because the account was: infostealer malware had captured the login of an employee of Italy’s Ministry of the Interior on its certified-email (PEC) system, and Hudson Rock found around 300 compromised logins for that domain in infostealer logs. The forged requests were sent to Revolut Bank UAB in Lithuania and, per the same reporting, ran for five to six months. American Banker reports the Lithuanian entity appears to have been chosen because EU law obliges it to answer European Investigation Orders, and that about 680 customers were affected — described as high-net-worth and crypto “whales” — none of them in the US. Revolut itself still says only “a limited number of customers” and has not named the agency. An actor calling itself IAmNotAVillain posted a $3M ransom demand on 16 September; Revolut says it “has not received any direct contact or demand”. Earlier reporting had cited a 10,000 BTC demand. The same actor claims 147 GB taken from an Italian law-enforcement agency, which Italian police are investigating. The 680-customer, five-month and $3M figures come from researchers and the actor, not from Revolut. What was handed over is unusually complete: names, dates of birth, occupations, addresses, email addresses and phone numbers, passport and driver’s licence copies, identity-verification selfies, statements with IBANs, and transaction and crypto history. Revolut says its systems and customer funds were not affected and that it has notified law enforcement, data-protection authorities and financial regulators. The new detail removes the comfortable reading of this incident. The request did not come from a look-alike domain that a careful analyst could have spotted; it came from a real government mailbox whose credentials were sitting in infostealer logs, so every check that validates the sender’s domain, headers or reference format would have passed. Law-enforcement request desks, subpoena and court-order intake, emergency disclosure requests and regulator information demands all end at a small team authorised to release exactly this dossier, and most verify the request rather than the requester. The control that works is an out-of-band callback to a number the institution already holds for that authority, dual authorisation before any bulk identity or transaction export, and volume alerting on the request desk itself — a single requester drawing hundreds of high-value customer files over months is a pattern somebody should have seen. It is also a reporting event: under DORA the initial notification clock for a major ICT-related incident runs in hours, and US banking institutions sit under a 36-hour supervisory notification rule, both starting from classification. Updated 21 September: the follow-on has arrived. Malwarebytes, reported by Infosecurity Magazine, has tracked a smishing campaign against Revolut customers running since 14 September, two days after the disclosure. The texts warn that the recipient must confirm their identity or lose access, and because they arrive inside the existing Revolut SMS conversation on the phone they read as a continuation of genuine bank messages. The link opens a page that asks for camera access and imitates Revolut’s live-video identity check before asking for the password — a convincing liveness step followed by a credential screen, which Malwarebytes says is enough to take over an account outright. Several hundred accounts are thought to have been affected, with high-net-worth crypto users singled out. American Banker’s 18 September account, updated 20 September, confirms the vector: credentials for Italy’s PEC certified-mail system tied to the Interior Ministry domain, believed bought from infostealer logs, used to send requests over several months to Revolut Bank UAB in Lithuania; Revolut has notified data-protection authorities and financial regulators, and the OCC’s conditional approval of its US national-bank charter earlier this month is contingent on information-security compliance. Whether the smishing list is the stolen dossier or an opportunistic mailing is not established, but the targeting matches. For every other institution the lesson is the sequence: a breach of customer contact data is followed, within days, by a campaign that impersonates the victim firm’s own security process, and any bank whose app uses a video or selfie check has just watched that check be faked. Updated 25 September — a second, separate incident: The Register reports that DriveWealth, a US brokerage that provides execution and clearing services, has notified customers that a “sophisticated social engineering campaign” on 4–5 September exposed historic customer data, and that the people affected include Revolut customers who used to trade US stocks through DriveWealth. Revolut moved its UK, EEA and Australian customers off that arrangement between December 2023 and June 2025, so the exposed records are legacy data. Per the notices as quoted, the data includes names, email and postal addresses, phone numbers, employment information, country of citizenship, age, gender and partial DriveWealth account numbers; passwords, payment and card details and bank account information were not affected. DriveWealth warns the data “could potentially be used for identity fraud, impersonation, further social engineering, or unsolicited contact”; Revolut says its own systems were not compromised and customer funds and investments remain safe. Neither company has given a count, DriveWealth’s website carried no public notice at the time of writing, and only one outlet has reported it so far — treat the detail as provisional. It is a different company and a different vector from the forged-request breach, but for the same customers it means a second set of contact and identity details in circulation in one month, which is exactly the raw material the smishing wave above needs. It is also a reminder that customer data held by a former brokerage, custody or banking-as-a-service partner does not leave your risk register when the customers migrate: ask former partners what they still retain and on what deletion schedule.

AWS says data held only in its Bahrain region cannot be recovered after drone strikes — the first permanent regional loss at a hyperscaler is a resilience test financial firms can no longer call hypothetical

Amazon Web Services has told customers that resources and data hosted exclusively in its Bahrain region, me-south-1, cannot be restored, and that one Availability Zone in its UAE region, me-central-1, is a total loss, Help Net Security reported on 17 September 2026 from AWS’s 15 September updates. The cause is physical rather than cyber: Iranian drone strikes that began in early March. AWS says two of its UAE facilities were directly struck; in Bahrain a nearby strike caused structural and power damage and fire suppression caused water damage, which AWS says exceeded what its redundancy design could withstand. A second Bahrain Availability Zone was disrupted in April, taking the region fully offline. AWS says most customers had already relocated using backups; restoration of the remaining UAE zones continues and it expects further Bahrain updates in early 2027. The FBI reported on 18 September 2026 that scams impersonating government officials produced 60,833 complaints to its Internet Crime Complaint Center between January 2025 and July 2026, with losses of $1.6 billion — an average of more than $26,000 per complaint. The breakdown shows how specific the pretexts have become. Jury-duty scams accounted for 6,833 complaints and about $36 million; professional-licence scams, in which a regulator appears to threaten the victim’s licence, 3,322 complaints and more than $37 million; and schemes aimed at ethnic communities and international students 1,809 complaints and more than $140 million, run over video calls staged with fake uniforms and offices. For banks, credit unions and payment firms these are the losses that surface at the teller window, in the contact centre and on the authorised-push-payment fraud ledger, and the victim is almost always sending the money willingly. The same week, Revolut’s breach showed the pretext working in the other direction, against an institution’s own compliance desk. Both depend on an authority claim that nobody verifies through a channel the fraudster does not control. The FBI’s categories also give fraud teams a ready typology list for the richer suspicious-activity reporting Treasury asked for earlier this month.

High Threat Actor

CrowdStrike profiles Slim Spider — a financially motivated actor that has learned how Brazilian instant payments and bank cloud actually work

CrowdStrike published research on 8 September 2026 on Slim Spider, a financially motivated adversary it has tracked against Brazilian financial institutions since at least March 2026. What distinguishes the group is domain knowledge rather than novel malware: it understands Pix instant payments, digital-asset platforms and the cloud services financial firms run underneath them, and it aims at the money rather than at resale data. In a late-March intrusion at a Brazilian bank, CrowdStrike observed custom Bash scripts querying cloud instance metadata to lift temporary cloud credentials, moving them out over socket connections, and then pursuing crypto assets and instant-payment accounts. The tradecraft is worth reading outside Brazil. Instance-metadata abuse is a generic cloud weakness — a workload that can reach the metadata endpoint can often mint short-lived credentials for whatever role it runs as, and those credentials rarely trigger the alerting that a stolen human password would. Pair that with an actor who already knows your payment initiation flow and your fraud thresholds, and the result is theft that looks like ordinary automated activity. UK, EU and US institutions running instant-payment schemes on cloud infrastructure face the same structural exposure even though this actor’s current targeting is regional.

High Threat Actor

Microsoft names two access brokers running helpdesk vishing into personal devices, then pulling SharePoint and Exchange out through the Graph API

Microsoft researchers, in reporting published by Dark Reading on 10 September 2026, detailed at least two initial-access-broker clusters — tracked as Storm-3032 and Storm-3121 — running voice-phishing campaigns since May 2026. The pattern is consistent: the caller impersonates the IT helpdesk, steers the target onto their own personal device rather than a managed one, and sends them to a lookalike Microsoft sign-in page on a plausible domain such as “company.add-passkey.com”. Credentials and session tokens are captured there. The brokers then abuse the Microsoft Graph API to enumerate and exfiltrate data from SharePoint, OneDrive and Exchange in small, periodic transfers rather than one bulk pull. Access is reportedly sold on to extortion groups including ShinyHunters. Microsoft says it has not yet tied these campaigns to a confirmed corporate breach, so the campaign is documented but no named victim is confirmed. The financial-services relevance is the helpdesk itself. Bank, insurer and broker service desks handle high volumes of urgent reset requests across branches, advisers and contractors, and the routing onto a personal device is deliberate — it sidesteps device compliance, conditional access and every endpoint control the firm actually owns. Because the exfiltration runs through Graph with a legitimate token, it will not look like malware on any endpoint; it looks like a user reading their own mail.

High Phishing

Two campaigns are working Microsoft 365 accounts at once — a million-message ACH and wire-fraud run, and passkey-themed social engineering that registers the attacker’s own passkey

Researchers described on 13 September 2026 two related campaigns against Microsoft 365 and connected cloud accounts. The first is volume fraud: more than a million scam emails aimed squarely at ACH and wire payments, the same accounts-payable and treasury workflows that Microsoft separately flagged on 11 September when it published new variations on invoice-fraud tradecraft aimed at finance teams. The second is more interesting and more dangerous. Rather than trying to defeat multi-factor authentication, attackers use passkey-themed lures — an enrolment prompt, a security-upgrade notice — to persuade the user to authorise a passkey that the attacker controls. The credential that results is legitimate, phishing-resistant by design, bound to the attacker’s device and durable in a way a stolen password never is. It survives a password reset. For financial institutions this lands on the two populations that matter most: the finance function, where a payment instruction changed inside a real mailbox looks like a real payment instruction, and privileged administrators, where a self-registered passkey is an authentication backdoor that no password-rotation exercise will close. The control is governance of the registration ceremony rather than the authentication one. Treat adding an authentication factor as a privileged action: require an existing strong factor or a verified device to enrol a new passkey, alert on enrolments outside managed devices and compliant locations, and audit the registered-credential list per user the way you audit privileged group membership. Then keep callback verification on payment-detail changes, because it is the one control that does not care whether the mailbox was genuine.

Fortinet confirms attacks on a FortiMail flaw that lets an unauthenticated attacker write files to the appliance — CVE-2026-104286 went into KEV with a three-day deadline, and Fortinet’s 5 October update now lists fixed releases

Fortinet published advisory FG-IR-26-175 on 1 October 2026 for CVE-2026-104286, a path-traversal flaw combined with faulty null-byte handling in FortiMail that lets an unauthenticated attacker write arbitrary files on the underlying system with crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests. Fortinet rates it CVSS 9.8 and says it has been reported exploited in the wild; CISA added it to KEV the same day with a federal deadline of 4 October. Affected releases are 7.2.0–7.2.9, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.6.0–7.6.6 and 8.0.0–8.0.1. At first the fixes were only planned; Fortinet’s update of 5 October lists 7.4.9, 7.6.7 and 8.0.2 as fixed and tells 7.2 customers to move to 7.4 or later. Its workarounds are to disable identity-based encryption (IBE), restrict webmail and management access to trusted networks, and block POST requests containing ../ to /ibe paths at a web application firewall. It also lists two attacker IP addresses and log patterns, including suspicious cron commands, unexplained administrator logouts and archive accounts set to send mail to remote destinations. Fortinet found the flaw internally and has not named an actor or victims. For banks and insurers the appliance is the mail gateway: IBE is the feature that delivers encrypted email to outside recipients through a web portal, and an attacker who can write files on the gateway can sit on inbound and outbound mail, including correspondence about payments.

Microsoft updated its advisory for CVE-2026-65660 on 25 September 2026 to say it had “reliable evidence of observed attacks”, and CISA added the flaw to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day with a federal remediation deadline of 28 September. A drive-by flaw in Thales SConnect, the browser middleware many institutions use for smart-card log-ins to SWIFT and banking portals, let a malicious web page load code on the workstation — CVE-2026-18397 was fixed in August and is not reported exploited

Researcher James Arnott of Bay Area Labs published details on 2 October 2026 of CVE-2026-18397 in SConnect, a browser extension and native helper from Thales DIS Czech Republic, formerly Gemalto, that lets web pages talk to smart cards and USB tokens. The researcher describes it as one of the main ways to authenticate to the SWIFT banking system, and Dark Reading reports more than a million Chrome users and use on banking, insurance and government portals. The flaw sits in a hand-rolled RSA-2048 check on the token that authorises a page to use the native helper: an oversized signature makes verification fail without writing its result buffer, and by spraying the heap a malicious page or iframe can make the stale buffer pass the check and get the helper to load a DLL, with no interaction beyond visiting the page. The researcher and Dark Reading give it a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.4. Thales was told on 29 June; fixed builds reached the Chrome Web Store and Apple App Store on 7 August, the extension was removed from the Edge store on 13 September, and the CVE was published on 1 October. No exploitation has been reported, and Thales did not respond to Dark Reading. The machines that run SConnect are among the most sensitive in a bank — SWIFT operator, treasury and trade-finance workstations — so the question is whether every one picked up the fix, including those on Edge, where the store listing simply disappeared.

The case goes back to the breach at American Medical Collection Agency, a debt collector, between 1 August 2018 and 30 March 2019, which affected more than 27.5 million people across AMCA’s clients, including more than 10.2 million Labcorp patients. Connecticut, Florida, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan and Texas led the investigation. The required measures include appointing a chief information security officer, an incident-response plan with provisions for vendor incidents, annual SOC 2 Type 2 audits of the debt collectors it uses, third-party assessments focused on vendor risk, limits on the data shared with vendors and security terms in vendor contracts. This is a settlement over a breach that is now seven years old, not a new incident, and Labcorp is a laboratory company rather than a lender. It is on this page because the breach happened at a collection agency: banks, card issuers and loan servicers that place accounts with collectors hold the same kind of vendor relationship, and here the attorneys general held the client responsible for the vendor’s failure.

CISA and NIST set numbers for cloud token and signing-key hygiene — one-hour tokens, 90-day key rotation and no tokens in logs

CISA and NIST published NIST IR 8587 on 15 September 2026, guidance on protecting the identity tokens and signing keys that cloud services use to decide who a caller is. The recommendations are specific: access and identity tokens valid for no more than one hour, with expired tokens rejected outright; signing keys rotated every 90 days for high-impact systems and at least annually otherwise; hardware-backed or isolated key storage, with signing done in isolated environments for high-impact systems; a mandatory audience claim; and tokens never written to logs. The document cites the 2020 SAML-forgery supply-chain attack and the incident in which forged tokens exposed more than 60,000 government emails. It is voluntary and aimed at federal agencies, cloud providers and cloud buyers, but financial institutions are among the heaviest buyers of cloud identity and run their own token issuers for open-banking APIs, mobile apps and partner integrations. This page carries WSO2 API Manager CVE-2026-5430, where a forged token is the whole attack, and the helpdesk-vishing and passkey campaigns, where a stolen session or rogue credential outlasts a password reset. Token lifetime, audience restriction and key custody are the settings that decide how far one forged or stolen token travels.

Treasury tells banks their scam reporting is not good enough — nearly $13 billion in cyber-enabled fraud losses reported since 2023

The US Treasury’s financial-crimes unit published guidance on 10 September 2026 urging banks to improve suspicious-activity reporting on cyber-enabled scams, citing close to $13 billion in cumulative losses reported since 2023 across romance and investment fraud, business email compromise and impersonation schemes. This is a supervisory advisory rather than an incident, and it should be read as one: the criticism is not that institutions are failing to file, it is that what they file is too thin to be useful downstream. A suspicious-activity report that records a payment as unusual without naming the scam typology, the contact channel, the beneficiary infrastructure or the associated addresses gives investigators nothing to join across institutions, which is exactly how the same mule network keeps working. The advisory lands in a week that illustrates the point twice over. The Revolut incident on this page was an impersonation scam run against a firm’s own compliance process, and the passkey and invoice-fraud campaigns elsewhere on this page are the delivery mechanism for a large share of the losses being counted. For a bank or payment firm the practical response is to check that the fraud, financial-crime and security functions are not each holding a separate third of the same story. The typology detail that makes a report useful — how the customer was contacted, what was impersonated, which platform hosted the approach — usually sits with the fraud team or the contact centre and never reaches the filing, while the technical indicators sit with security and reach nobody.
