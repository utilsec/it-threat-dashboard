UNISOC modem flaws chain to kernel-privilege code execution triggered over a VoLTE video call — no vendor fix available

Researchers chained multiple flaws in UNISOC modem firmware to run code with kernel privileges from the modem context, taking advantage of insufficient separation between modem memory and kernel memory on affected chipsets. The chain is reached over a VoLTE video call, which places the trigger on the operator’s own IMS signalling path rather than on anything the subscriber installs or clicks. Handsets named in the research include the Xiaomi Redmi A5, the Motorola E13 and the Realme C33 — budget devices sold in high volume, particularly in emerging markets, where a single model can account for a meaningful share of an operator’s active fleet. The work is proof-of-concept and there are no reports of exploitation in the wild. Remediation is the unresolved part: at publication no vendor firmware fix existed and UNISOC had not responded to the disclosure, so there is currently nothing for carriers or handset makers to push to devices. That leaves network-side measures as the only available lever — IMS and VoLTE abuse monitoring for anomalous video-call session setup, rate limiting and screening of malformed signalling, and an accurate inventory of which UNISOC-based models are active on the network so exposure can be quantified now and remediated when firmware eventually ships.

Info Incident

T-Mobile expelled Salt Typhoon by driving to a data centre and cutting a cable — the intruder was sitting on another carrier’s router

T-Mobile’s security chief, Jeff Simon, is on the record describing how the carrier handled the 2024 Salt Typhoon campaign: analysts traced anomalous behaviour back to a router belonging to a different telecom company, and the response was to drive to a data centre near the Bellevue headquarters and physically snip the cable. T-Mobile went on to avoid the large-scale compromise that hit AT&T, Verizon and other US carriers in the same campaign. The reporting does not establish that the cut alone was decisive, and the underlying intrusion set is two years old, so this belongs on the page as an operational case study rather than fresh threat activity. What earns it space is where the adversary was standing. Salt Typhoon did not have to breach T-Mobile directly; it needed a foothold at an interconnect partner whose equipment already sat inside a trusted path. Every operator carries dozens of those relationships — peering, transit, roaming, wholesale, managed-service handoffs — and most are governed by commercial agreements that say nothing useful about who may disconnect whom, at what hour, on whose authority. The technical lesson is that logical containment across an interconnect can be slower than a drive across town. The governance lesson is sharper: the decision to sever a partner’s circuit had to be improvised, because nobody had written down in advance who was allowed to make it.

Critical Advisory

Three CVSS 10.0 flaws in Cisco Crosswork land in the carrier OSS layer — unexploited so far, but this is the software that drives the network

Cisco has published fixes for nine issues across Crosswork and Secure Workload. On the Crosswork side, versions up to and including 7.2.1 carry CVE-2026-20030 (SQL injection), CVE-2026-20357 (missing authentication) and CVE-2026-20358 (filesystem control), all rated CVSS 10.0, plus CVE-2026-20359 at 9.9; the fix is 7.2.1-SP. Secure Workload is patched separately in 3.10.9.1 and 4.0.4.16 and carries two further 10.0 issues, CVE-2026-20315 and CVE-2026-20317. Cisco states none of these are known to be exploited, and no proof-of-concept has been reported, so the honest framing is a patch window rather than an incident. The reason it lands harder for operators than for a general enterprise is what Crosswork is. Data Gateway, Network Controller and Planning are service-provider automation: they hold device credentials, collect streaming telemetry from the transport estate, and push configuration change across it. Missing authentication on a platform of that kind is not a server compromise, it is credentialed control of the network the platform manages, with a ready-made channel for pushing change that looks entirely legitimate downstream. That is precisely the position state-aligned telecom intrusion sets have sought in recent years, so the window between advisory and weaponisation is the thing to spend this week on.

High Campaign

Operation CameraSwarm took 14,530 Dahua devices in 35 days — and 283 of them were behind NAT

Hunt.io has documented a single operator compromising more than 14,530 Dahua devices between 17 June and 22 July 2026. Three methods ran in parallel. Credential attacks, sourced from 12,324 unique IP addresses, did the bulk of the work. Authentication-bypass exploitation of CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 — both CVSS 9.8, both long-standing CISA KEV entries — accounted for 1,923 cameras. A third path abused the vendor’s peer-to-peer relay service to reach 283 devices that had no inbound exposure at all. Compromises concentrated in Ukraine and Russia, and artifacts point to a Russian-speaking operator, though no vendor has made a formal attribution. For carriers the P2P figure is the one to sit with. Those 283 devices were behind NAT, which is the assumption most subscriber-side security rests on: nothing is port-forwarded, therefore nothing is reachable. A vendor cloud relay dissolves that boundary, and the traffic leaves the subscriber line looking like an ordinary outbound session to the manufacturer. The rest of the estate matters commercially rather than technically: compromised cameras on your access network are your address space, your abuse desk, your reputation blocks, and eventually your DDoS capacity pointed at your own customers. Five-year-old KEV flaws being productive at this scale is a statement about CPE patch reality, not about the flaws.

Medium Campaign

Android car head units are being enrolled into a residential-proxy botnet through their own OEM update channel

Researchers have confirmed malware spreading to Android-based automotive head units through the units’ built-in manufacturer update mechanism, monetised through ad fraud and by selling the infected devices as residential-proxy capacity. No CVE has been assigned, and no attribution has been published. The delivery path is the story: the compromise arrives over the channel the device is designed to trust implicitly, which means there is no user action to blame, no misconfiguration to correct at the subscriber end, and no patch a fleet operator can apply independently of the OEM. For a carrier this is a data-plan problem wearing a car’s clothes. Connected-vehicle head units sit on embedded SIMs and machine-to-machine tariffs sold by mobile operators, which places the resulting proxy exit nodes inside mobile ASNs and bills the fraud traffic to carrier-issued subscriptions. That has two consequences worth separating. The first is commercial: unexplained data consumption on M2M lines that were provisioned on the assumption of predictable, low-volume telemetry. The second is reputational, and slower to reverse — mobile IP space carries unusual trust in fraud-scoring and bot-detection systems precisely because it is hard to rent, and a proxy network built on connected-vehicle plans erodes that for every other subscriber sharing the range. M2M and IoT tariffs are typically the least-monitored product line an operator runs.

CISA adds four actively exploited flaws to KEV — a Windows IKE extensions double free tops the list for carrier interconnect

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 18 August with a 21 August federal remediation deadline. The one with the most direct carrier relevance is CVE-2026-33824, a double free in the Microsoft Windows IKE service extensions (IKEEXT) that leads to remote code execution: IKE is the key-exchange half of IPsec, which carriers rely on for interconnect tunnels, site-to-site links and managed enterprise VPN services, so Windows hosts terminating or brokering those tunnels are squarely in scope. The other three additions are CVE-2026-59310 in Broadcom VMware vCenter (path traversal to code execution), CVE-2026-55040 in Microsoft SharePoint (weak authentication allowing a security-feature bypass) and CVE-2026-65400 in Apple macOS Screen Sharing (improper authentication). All four are listed as exploited in the wild.

VMware vCenter path-traversal RCE under mass exploitation, now added to CISA KEV

Broadcom disclosed this directory-traversal flaw in the vCenter Server syslog service in VMSA-2026-0006 on 29 July, CVSS 9.8, with no workaround available. Victims began connecting to attacker infrastructure on 3 August, and by 5 August roughly 95% of an eventual 361 victim IPs across 47 countries had appeared, concentrated in Germany, the United States, Turkey, Iran and France. Many carriers run virtualised network functions, 4G/5G control- and user-plane elements and OSS/BSS platforms on vCenter-managed clusters, so a compromise at the hypervisor management layer reaches far beyond any single workload.

Congressional report finds Chinese state carriers still present at critical US internet nodes despite revoked operating authority

A 49-page report from a US House select committee concludes that China Mobile, China Unicom and China Telecom retain a presence at critical nodes of US internet infrastructure even though the FCC revoked their Section 214 common-carrier authorisations. The technical finding is that the three operators shifted into network services that fall outside those revoked authorisations — less-regulated interconnection, transit, data-centre and cloud-adjacent arrangements — so the physical and logical presence at exchange points persists while the licensing hook does not. The report connects that infrastructure to observed routing incidents and to the Salt Typhoon intrusions into US telecom networks, and its recommendations include broader FCC authority and a mandate to remove and replace the affected equipment and services. Nothing here is an incident disclosure; it is a mapping exercise that carriers can use against their own dependency inventories.
